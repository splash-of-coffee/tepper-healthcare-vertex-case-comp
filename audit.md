# Audit of Agent Prompt v3 and Supporting Files — 2026 Tepper Healthcare Case Competition (Vertex / inaxaplin)

Written 2026-09-27. Scope: `prompts\agent-prompt-v3.md`, `prompts\analytics-scientist-brief.md`, the four files in `notes\`, the case PDF, the announcement, the organizer email, all seven analyst reports, and the Tepper case-competition workshop notes. Web checks were run on public facts only. No case or report content was sent to any external service.

Page references to analyst reports use the PDF page number. The Jefferies report prints its own page numbers, which run 7 lower than the PDF page (printed page 73 is PDF page 80).

---

## 1. Verdict

I estimate that this prompt, run as written, has about a 15% chance of producing the first-place deck and about a 45% chance of reaching the Boston final. Both numbers are judgment estimates, because the number of competing teams and their quality are unknown. The process design is stronger than a typical student effort, but the fact base leaves out what Vertex already does for this disease (the "Power Forward" patient campaign, running since November 2022, and free APOL1 testing through Labcorp, Natera, and Arkana), treats the 150K and 100K patient figures as possibly US-only when the sponsor's own reports say they cover the US and Europe, and gives the model no way to separate patients found earlier from patients found only because of the investment. The three changes that most raise the odds are to rebuild the baseline and the fact seed around Vertex's current programs and current facts, to add diagnosis pull-forward, approval probability and timing, the biopsy and prior-authorization step, and driver-based scenarios to the model specification, and to cut the five hard-stop checkpoints to three time-boxed ones with the theme choice made non-blocking. With those three changes I would raise the estimates to about 30% for first place and about 60% for the final.

## 4. Gaps: what a winning submission needs that no file asks for

1. **Diagnosis pull-forward.** The model must separate patients diagnosed earlier from patients found only because of the investment, and credit each correctly (see F05). No file mentions this.
2. **The label-eligible share of primary care.** The first label likely covers non-diabetic, proteinuric AMKD with eGFR at or above the trial floor. The team needs an estimate of how many US patients meet that definition and what share sees only a primary care physician. The whole primary care case depends on this number.
3. **Nephrology capacity.** If primary care testing creates referrals, nephrology wait times and biopsy capacity cap the number of patients who start therapy. The deck needs a referral design (for example, e-consults or a fast-track referral path) inside the chosen option.
4. **Payer mix of added patients.** Patients found through FQHCs and safety-net clinics carry more Medicaid and 340B volume and a lower net price than the brand average.
5. **The orphan and IRA interaction.** The 2025 change to the orphan exclusion creates an upside for an orphan-only label and a trade-off for label expansion.
6. **A response curve for headcount and budget.** The case asks for a headcount; the judges will ask why that number. A marginal-return curve answers it.
7. **Analog evidence from primary care launches.** ATTR-CM in Black Americans, SGLT2 and finerenone kidney promotion to primary care, and sponsored genetic-testing programs before and after drug approval give the testing-uplift and response assumptions an external anchor.
8. **Family cascade testing.** Each confirmed patient has relatives with elevated odds of carrying two variants, and a family-history code (Z84.11) now exists. Cascade testing through the confirmed patient's nephrologist or primary care physician is a high-yield channel that fits inside either option.
9. **Stage-gate triggers that protect the final round.** Because the strategy cannot change after Oct 8, the recommendation should state in advance how it scales with the AMPLITUDE interim result and the approval path. The final deck can then add detail without changing the strategy.
10. **A patient-pathway page.** The Kellogg source lists storytelling as a winning factor, and the writing standard bans narrative devices. One slide that shows a typical patient's path from a primary care visit to a nephrology diagnosis, with the years lost at each step and each step sourced, gives the read deck a human anchor without inventing a person. Label any composite as illustrative.
11. **Deadline logistics.** Submitter, backup submitter, upload method, and file naming (see F24).
12. **Questions for the organizers, sent today.** Whether the appendix counts toward 15 pages, whether design elements such as EHR prompts or cascade testing may sit inside an option, whether outside readers may review the deck, and whether the organizers want an AI disclosure. The office hours on Sept 22 have passed, and the office-hours placeholder in `<inputs>` is still empty.
13. **A human read before submission.** One reader from outside the team, if the rules allow it, catches what three simulated judges from the same model cannot.
