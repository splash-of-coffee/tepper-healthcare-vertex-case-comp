# Acronyms and Terms — Vertex / AMKD Case

Written 2026-09-27 as the team's plain-English reference. The "Why it matters here" column explains how each term connects to this case. Vertex program names and stages come from the portfolio slide in the case PDF (Q1 2026 earnings, May 2026). Definitions of general industry terms are standard usage. The agent adds any new acronym it introduces and checks the deck against this file.

## 2. Diseases and conditions

| Term | Meaning | Why it matters here |
|---|---|---|
| **AMKD** | APOL1-mediated kidney disease: kidney disease caused by carrying two high-risk copies of the APOL1 gene. | This is the disease in the case. |
| **APOL1** | Apolipoprotein L1, a gene. Its G1 and G2 variants protect against African sleeping sickness but damage the kidney when a person carries two copies. | The genetic test for these variants is the diagnosis bottleneck. |
| **G1 / G2** | The two high-risk APOL1 variants. G1/G1, G2/G2, and G1/G2 are the high-risk genotypes. | Carrying two variants is required for an AMKD diagnosis. |
| **CKD** | Chronic kidney disease: kidney damage or reduced function lasting more than three months, graded in stages 1–5. | AMKD patients currently receive standard CKD care. |
| **ESKD / ESRD** | End-stage kidney (or renal) disease: kidney failure that needs dialysis or a transplant. | This is the costly outcome that treatment tries to prevent. |
| **FSGS** | Focal segmental glomerulosclerosis, a scarring pattern in the kidney's filters. It is strongly linked to APOL1. | Many AMKD patients carry an FSGS diagnosis. |
| **HIVAN** | HIV-associated nephropathy, a kidney disease strongly linked to APOL1. | Part of the APOL1 disease spectrum. |
| **Acromegaly** | A hormone disorder in which the body makes too much growth hormone. | Palsonify's disease. |
| **CAH** | Congenital adrenal hyperplasia, an inherited hormone disorder of the adrenal glands. | Atumelnant's disease. |
| **IgAN** | IgA nephropathy, an autoimmune kidney disease. | Povetacicept's first indication and the reason Vertex already has nephrology reps. |
| **pMN** | Primary membranous nephropathy, an autoimmune kidney disease. | Povetacicept's second kidney indication, in pivotal trials. |
| **ADPKD** | Autosomal dominant polycystic kidney disease, an inherited disease in which cysts grow in the kidneys. | VX-407's target. |
| **CF** | Cystic fibrosis, a genetic lung and digestive disease. | Vertex's largest business. |
| **CFTR** | Cystic fibrosis transmembrane conductance regulator, the protein that is faulty in CF. | The target of Vertex's CF drugs. |
| **SCD** | Sickle cell disease. | Casgevy's indication. |
| **TDT** | Transfusion-dependent beta thalassemia. | Casgevy's other indication. |
| **T1D / T2D** | Type 1 diabetes (autoimmune) and type 2 diabetes (metabolic). | T1D is the zimislecel target. T2D was one of the AMPLIFIED cohorts. |
| **DPN** | Diabetic peripheral neuropathy: nerve pain in the feet and legs caused by diabetes. | A primary-care-heavy indication for suzetrigine. |
| **DM1** | Myotonic dystrophy type 1, a genetic muscle disease. | VX-670's target. |
| **wAIHA** | Warm autoimmune hemolytic anemia. | A povetacicept indication in Phase 1/2. |
| **gMG** | Generalized myasthenia gravis, an autoimmune neuromuscular disease. | A povetacicept indication in Phase 1/2. |
| **HCV** | Hepatitis C virus. | Incivek's market. |

## 3. Kidney tests and clinical measures

| Term | Meaning | Why it matters here |
|---|---|---|
| **eGFR** | Estimated glomerular filtration rate: how well the kidneys filter, estimated from a blood test. Lower means worse. | "eGFR slope," the rate of decline over time, is an AMPLITUDE endpoint. |
| **Proteinuria** | Protein in the urine, a sign of kidney filter damage. | AMPLITUDE requires heavy proteinuria; AMPLIFIED studies modest proteinuria. |
| **UACR** | Urine albumin-to-creatinine ratio, a urine test for protein leakage. PCPs can order it cheaply. | Used in AMPLIFIED results. A high UACR in a Black patient could trigger an APOL1 test. |
| **UPCR** | Urine protein-to-creatinine ratio, a similar urine protein test. | Used in AMPLIFIED results and trial entry criteria. |
| **Genotyping / genetic test** | A lab test (blood or saliva) that reads a person's APOL1 variants. | The diagnosis bottleneck; "genetic testing uplift" is a required case output. |
| **ACEi / ARB** | ACE inhibitors and angiotensin receptor blockers, blood pressure drugs that protect the kidneys. | The current standard of care for AMKD. |
| **RAAS** | Renin-angiotensin-aldosterone system, the hormone system that ACEi and ARBs act on. | Background term. |
| **SGLT2i** | SGLT2 inhibitors (for example Farxiga, Jardiance), diabetes drugs that also slow CKD. | Their makers already promote kidney care to PCPs, which makes them a precedent and a competitor for PCP attention. |
| **KDIGO** | Kidney Disease: Improving Global Outcomes, the organization that publishes kidney care guidelines. | Guideline adoption of APOL1 testing would change testing rates. |
| **ICD-10** | The standard diagnosis coding system used on medical claims. | AMKD now has specific ICD-10 codes, which make it possible to find patients in claims data and target PCPs. |
| **N07.B / Z84.11** | The ICD-10 codes for AMKD (N07.B) and for a family history of AMKD (Z84.11), effective Oct 1, 2025. | N07.B finds only patients already diagnosed. Z84.11 supports family testing. |
| **Cascade testing** | Testing the relatives of a patient who has a genetic diagnosis. | A possible design element inside an option; it must use patient-initiated contact, documented consent, and state genetic-testing law. |
| **Biopsy** | Removal of a small piece of kidney tissue to examine under a microscope, done through a nephrologist. | Payers may require biopsy findings before covering inaxaplin, which makes nephrology referral a funnel step. |
| **NKF / ASN** | National Kidney Foundation / American Society of Nephrology. In 2021 they jointly removed race from the eGFR equation. | Wording about race and kidney function on the slides must reflect that decision. |

## 4. Clinical trials and regulation

| Term | Meaning | Why it matters here |
|---|---|---|
| **FDA** | US Food and Drug Administration. | It approves inaxaplin and regulates drug advertising. |
| **Phase 1 / 2 / 3** | Clinical trial stages: safety, then early efficacy, then large confirmatory trials. | Each program's stage signals its risk. |
| **Pivotal trial** | The trial whose results support approval. | AMPLITUDE is pivotal. |
| **POC** | Proof of concept: early evidence that a drug works. | AMPLIFIED is a POC study. |
| **IA** | Interim analysis: a pre-planned look at trial data before the trial ends. | The AMPLITUDE IA in early 2027 is the next major milestone. |
| **Endpoint** | The measurement a trial uses to judge success. | AMPLITUDE uses eGFR slope and proteinuria change. |
| **Accelerated approval** | FDA approval based on an early marker likely to predict benefit, with a confirmatory trial required afterward. | The likely path for inaxaplin, which sets the launch timing. |
| **NDA / BLA** | New Drug Application (small molecules) / Biologics License Application (biologics): the filings that request FDA approval. | Inaxaplin needs an NDA; povetacicept filed a BLA. |
| **PDUFA date** | The FDA's target date to decide on an application (named for the Prescription Drug User Fee Act). | Povetacicept's is Nov 30, 2026. |
| **Label** | The FDA-approved description of who a drug is for. | The label decides which patients count in the model. |
| **Orphan drug designation** | FDA status for drugs treating fewer than 200,000 US patients. It brings incentives and exclusivity. | Primary AMKD, at about 150K patients, may qualify. The agent checks. |
| **LOE** | Loss of exclusivity: when patents or exclusivity end and generics can enter. | This sets the model horizon and explains CF concentration risk. |
| **IRA** | Inflation Reduction Act (2022). Among other things, it lets Medicare negotiate prices for small molecules 9 years after approval and for biologics after 13. | A long-term risk to inaxaplin revenue. |
| **GINA** | Genetic Information Nondiscrimination Act (2008). It bans genetic discrimination in health insurance and employment, but not in life, disability, or long-term-care insurance. | Central to patient trust in genetic testing. |
| **MLR** | Medical-Legal-Regulatory review: the internal review every promotional piece must pass. | Constrains DTC and rep messaging. |
| **Unbranded vs. branded** | Unbranded materials discuss a disease without naming a drug and are allowed before approval. Branded materials name the drug and are allowed only after approval. | Sets what Option 2 can do before launch. |

## 5. Commercial and market access

| Term | Meaning | Why it matters here |
|---|---|---|
| **GTM** | Go-to-market: the plan for reaching customers. | The case asks for a GTM strategy. |
| **HCP** | Healthcare professional (a physician, nurse practitioner, physician assistant, etc.). | The audience for sales reps. |
| **PCP** | Primary care physician (family medicine, internal medicine). | The setting where most undiagnosed AMKD patients are managed. |
| **NP / PA** | Nurse practitioner / physician assistant. | They see many primary care patients and can order tests, so they are possible targets. |
| **FQHC** | Federally qualified health center: a community clinic serving underserved populations. | Likely sites for many at-risk patients. |
| **340B** | A federal program that lets qualifying safety-net providers buy drugs at large discounts. | Patients found through safety-net clinics carry lower net prices, so the model applies net price by payer channel. |
| **E-consult** | A written consultation in which a primary care physician asks a specialist a question through the health record, without a full referral visit. | A possible referral design element inside the chosen option. |
| **EHR prompt** | An alert inside the electronic health record that suggests an action, such as an APOL1 test for an eligible patient. | A possible design element inside an option; the organizers allow it if it follows the law. |
| **AKF** | American Kidney Fund, a kidney patient charity. Vertex funds its APOL1 awareness campaign. | Part of the baseline. |
| **IDN** | Integrated delivery network: a health system that owns hospitals and physician practices. | Reaching one IDN can reach many PCPs through system-wide protocols. |
| **FTE** | Full-time equivalent, meaning one full-time employee. | Option 1a hires FTE reps. |
| **CSO** | Contract sales organization: a company that rents out trained reps (for example Syneos, IQVIA, Inizio). | Option 1b. |
| **Rep / field force** | Sales representatives who visit doctors in person. | Options 1a, 1b, and 1c all change the field force. |
| **Call / detail** | One rep visit to a doctor to discuss a drug or disease. | Headcount is sized from the calls needed per target per year. |
| **Reach / frequency** | Reach is how many target doctors are visited; frequency is how often each one is visited. | The main inputs for sizing the sales options. |
| **Decile** | Doctors ranked into ten groups by patient volume or potential. "Top-decile PCPs" are the highest-potential ones. | Option 1c's "top primary care targets." |
| **Territory** | The geographic area one rep covers. | Headcount follows the number of territories. |
| **KAM** | Key account manager: a rep who handles health systems rather than individual doctors. | A possible design element for IDNs. |
| **MSL** | Medical science liaison: a scientific, non-sales role that talks with doctors about the science. MSLs are allowed before approval. | Pre-launch education is done by MSLs. |
| **KOL** | Key opinion leader: an influential physician expert. | Nephrology KOLs shape PCP behavior. |
| **DTC / DTP** | Direct-to-consumer / direct-to-patient advertising (TV, digital, social, community). | Option 2. |
| **HUB** | A patient support service that handles insurance, copay, and starting therapy. | Affects how many prescriptions become patients on therapy. |
| **PBM** | Pharmacy benefit manager: the company that manages drug coverage for insurers (for example CVS Caremark, Express Scripts, Optum). | It controls access and rebates. |
| **Payer** | Whoever pays for the drug: a commercial insurer, Medicare, or Medicaid. | Coverage of both the drug and the genetic test matters. |
| **PA** | Prior authorization: insurer approval required before a drug is covered. | Friction that lowers conversion. |
| **Formulary** | An insurer's list of covered drugs. | Access assumption. |
| **WAC** | Wholesale acquisition cost: the list price. | The starting point for net price. |
| **GTN** | Gross-to-net: the gap between list price and the net price the company keeps after rebates and discounts. | Net revenue = gross revenue × (1 − GTN %). |
| **NBRx / TRx** | New-to-brand prescriptions / total prescriptions. | Launch KPIs. |
| **Persistence / adherence** | Persistence is whether patients stay on the drug over time; adherence is whether they take it as prescribed. | These reduce treated patient-years in the model. |
| **Patient funnel** | The chain of steps from at-risk person to treated patient: at risk → tested → diagnosed → referred → prescribed → on therapy → persistent. | The backbone of the model. |
| **Share of voice** | A brand's share of total promotion in its category. | Competing for PCP attention against SGLT2 promotion. |

## 6. Finance and valuation

| Term | Meaning | Why it matters here |
|---|---|---|
| **NPV** | Net present value: future cash flows converted to today's dollars at a discount rate, minus the investment. Positive means the investment creates value. | A required case output. |
| **rNPV** | Risk-adjusted NPV: cash flows multiplied by the probability that the drug is approved and succeeds. | Useful because inaxaplin is not yet approved. |
| **Pull-forward** | A patient diagnosed earlier because of the program who would have been diagnosed later anyway. | The model credits only the added treated years for these patients, not their full lifetime revenue. |
| **Response curve** | How added prescriptions change as headcount or budget rises; each added rep or dollar usually adds less than the one before. | Used to choose headcount and budget, and to answer "why this many reps?" |
| **ROI** | Return on investment: gain divided by cost. The team must state which version it uses. | A required case output. |
| **Payback period** | How long until cumulative cash flow turns positive. It can be simple or discounted. | A required case output. |
| **IRR** | Internal rate of return: the discount rate at which NPV equals zero. | An optional extra metric. |
| **WACC** | Weighted average cost of capital: the discount rate for Vertex's cash flows. | The discount rate in the model. |
| **PoS / PTRS** | Probability of success / probability of technical and regulatory success. | Used in rNPV and in analyst models. |
| **Peak sales** | A drug's highest annual revenue. | Analysts quote it for inaxaplin. |
| **Incremental** | The difference between a scenario and the baseline. | Every return in this case must be incremental to a nephrology-only launch. |
| **Contribution margin** | Revenue minus variable costs such as manufacturing and royalties. | Converts revenue into profit in the model. |
| **COGS** | Cost of goods sold. | Low for small-molecule pills, which gives high margins. |
| **SG&A** | Selling, general, and administrative expense. Sales force and advertising costs sit here. | Where the case's investment shows up. |
| **Opex / Capex** | Operating expense / capital expenditure. | Sales and advertising spend is opex. |
| **EV** | Enterprise value: market cap plus debt minus cash. | Used in peer comparisons (EV/revenue). |
| **Market cap** | Share price × shares outstanding. | Vertex's has been above $100B. |
| **Sensitivity / tornado chart** | Tests how much the result moves when one input changes. A tornado chart ranks the inputs by impact. | Shows which assumptions matter most. |
| **Breakeven** | The value of a driver at which NPV equals zero (for example, the incremental patients needed). | A simple way to show how much room the investment has before it loses money. |
| **Base / bull / bear case** | The expected, optimistic, and pessimistic scenarios. | Anchored to the overview, bull, and bear analyst reports respectively. |
| **PT** | Price target: an analyst's forecast share price. | Appears in the analyst reports. |
| **OW / EW / UW** | Overweight / equal-weight / underweight: analyst ratings meaning roughly buy, hold, or sell. | Appears in the analyst reports. |

## 7. Case and consulting shorthand

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **Option 1a / 1b / 1c** | Sales options: 1a is Expansion (a hired FTE primary care team), 1b is Contracting (a CSO primary care team), 1c is Retargeting (the existing nephrology reps add top PCPs). Recommend at most one. |
| **Option 2** | The marketing option: expanded direct-to-patient advertising to drive APOL1 testing. |
| **Baseline** | The launch using only the existing nephrology sales force, with no primary care push. Every return is measured against it. |
| **MECE** | Mutually exclusive, collectively exhaustive: splitting a problem into parts that do not overlap and together cover everything. |
| **Pyramid principle** | Answer first, then the supporting reasons, then the evidence. |
| **Ghost deck** | A deck of slide titles only, used to test the storyline before building slides. |
| **Action title** | A slide title that states the slide's conclusion as a full sentence. |
| **SCR** | Situation, complication, resolution: a storyline structure. |
| **Stage gate** | A pre-set checkpoint where the plan scales up, holds, or stops based on results. |
| **KPI** | Key performance indicator: a metric used to track whether the plan is working. |
