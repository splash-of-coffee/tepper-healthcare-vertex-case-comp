## 2. Diseases and conditions

| Term | Business meaning | Technical meaning | Why it matters here |
|---|---|---|---|
| **AMKD** | AMKD is a kidney disease caused by genes a person inherits. It occurs mostly in people with recent African ancestry, and no drug treats it today; inaxaplin would be the first. | APOL1-mediated kidney disease: proteinuric CKD in a person with two APOL1 risk variants (G1/G1, G2/G2, or G1/G2) in whom APOL1 drives the injury. Biopsy patterns include FSGS and collapsing glomerulopathy. ICD-10-CM code N07.B since Oct 1, 2025. | The case states that 13% of African Americans carry two variants and that 20% of those develop AMKD. Vertex puts primary AMKD at about 150,000 people in the US and Europe combined. |
| **APOL1** | APOL1 is a gene and the blood protein it makes. The protein kills a parasite that causes African sleeping sickness. People with two "risk" copies of the gene make a form of the protein that can injure the kidney. | Apolipoprotein L1, made mainly in the liver and carried on HDL particles as part of trypanosome lytic factor; podocytes and blood-vessel cells also make it. The leading model holds that risk-variant APOL1 made inside kidney cells forms cation channels in cell membranes and injures podocytes. Inaxaplin binds APOL1 and blocks that channel function (Egbuna et al., NEJM 2023). | Diagnosis requires a genotype test, which primary care rarely orders (case PDF). One risk copy adds little or no kidney risk; two copies are required for AMKD. |
| **G1 / G2** | G1 and G2 are the two risky versions of the APOL1 gene. A person who inherits two risky copies, one from each parent, is the person who can develop AMKD. | The two high-risk APOL1 variants. G1/G1, G2/G2, and G1/G2 are the high-risk genotypes. | Carrying two variants is required for an AMKD diagnosis. |
| **Primary AMKD** | Primary AMKD is AMKD with no other explanation for the kidney damage, such as diabetes. It is the patient group in Vertex's main trial, so it is the group a first approval would most likely cover. | Two APOL1 risk variants plus proteinuric kidney disease with no other known cause. AMPLITUDE admits UPCR 0.7 to under 10 g/g, eGFR 25 to under 75 mL/min/1.73 m², no diabetes, no transplant, and ages 10–65 (Morgan Stanley p.12; Jefferies PDF p.71, p.77). | Vertex's figure of about 150,000 covers the US and Europe combined (Sept 22, 2026 release). WS-A must estimate the US-only count. |
| **NDKD** | NDKD is kidney disease in a person who does not have diabetes. Diabetes is the most common cause of kidney disease, so its absence points doctors toward other causes, such as APOL1. | Non-diabetic kidney disease. Oppenheimer (p.10), citing a global genotyping study, reports that 21–31% of NDKD patients and 44–50% of FSGS patients carry two APOL1 risk variants; the study population is not stated. | These shares are candidate test-positive rates for the funnel. WS-A must confirm the population they describe before the model uses them. |
| **Trypanosome** (African sleeping sickness) | A trypanosome is a single-celled parasite that tsetse flies spread in Africa, where it causes sleeping sickness. The APOL1 variants that raise kidney risk became common because they helped people survive this infection. | Trypanosoma brucei. Normal APOL1 kills T. b. brucei; T. b. rhodesiense, found in East Africa, resists it, and G1 and G2 APOL1 kill T. b. rhodesiense in lab tests (Genovese et al., Science 2010). The variants are most common in West Africa, home of T. b. gambiense; which parasite drove their spread is not settled (check). | The biology explains why the variants cluster in people with recent African ancestry, including Afro-Caribbean and Hispanic or Latino people. Slides should name ancestry, not race, as the risk factor. |
| **Podocyte** | Podocytes are the cells that wrap the kidney's tiny filters and keep protein in the blood. When they are damaged, protein leaks into the urine, and the body cannot replace the lost cells. | Terminally differentiated epithelial cells on the outer surface of glomerular capillaries; the glomerulus is the kidney's filtering unit. Their foot processes and slit diaphragms form the last barrier to protein. Podocyte loss causes proteinuria and FSGS scarring. | Inaxaplin aims to stop risk-variant APOL1 from injuring podocytes (Oppenheimer p.11). The trials measure proteinuria because it tracks podocyte injury. |
| **Penetrance** | Penetrance is the share of people with a risk genotype who develop the disease. Most people with two APOL1 risk copies never develop kidney disease, which makes testing harder to justify to patients. | The proportion of carriers of a genotype who show the disease. APOL1 high-risk genotypes show incomplete penetrance: the case gives 20%, and Jefferies' model uses 20%, citing Pollak 2023 (PDF p.80). | Lifetime US risk is about 1.2 million people (47M × 13% × 20%). That lifetime figure must never be compared with current or label-eligible counts. |
| **Second hit** | A second hit is a trigger, often an infection, that sets off kidney disease in someone who already carries two APOL1 risk copies. It helps explain why most carriers stay healthy. | A two-hit model: the high-risk genotype is necessary but not sufficient, and an inflammatory trigger such as interferon from HIV or another viral infection raises APOL1 production and starts disease. Jefferies' expert cites infection or cytokine surges (PDF p.79). | HIV-associated nephropathy is the clearest example. Judges may ask why only 20% of carriers develop AMKD. |
| **APOL1-FSGS** | APOL1-FSGS is scarring in the kidney's filters, seen on a biopsy, in a person with two APOL1 risk copies. It is the group in which inaxaplin produced its first results. | Focal segmental glomerulosclerosis in a person with two APOL1 risk variants. FDA granted inaxaplin Breakthrough Therapy and Rare Pediatric Disease designations for APOL1-mediated FSGS (Vertex release, Mar 16, 2023; Vertex FY2025 10-K, via WS-H). | The Phase 2a study cut UPCR 47.6% in 13 patients (Morgan Stanley p.6). Stifel and William Blair question benefit outside APOL1-FSGS (Stifel p.1; William Blair p.1–2). |
| **CKD** | Chronic kidney disease means the kidneys have been damaged or working poorly for at least three months. AMKD is one cause of it. | Chronic kidney disease: kidney damage or reduced function lasting more than three months, graded in stages 1–5. | AMKD patients currently receive standard CKD care. |
| **ESKD / ESRD** | End-stage kidney disease (also called end-stage renal disease) means the kidneys have failed and the patient needs dialysis or a transplant to live. Treatment aims to prevent it. | End-stage kidney (or renal) disease: kidney failure that needs dialysis or a transplant. | This is the costly outcome that treatment tries to prevent. |
| **FSGS** | FSGS is a pattern of scarring in the kidney's tiny filters that a doctor sees under a microscope after a biopsy. Two APOL1 risk copies are a major cause of it in Black patients. | Focal segmental glomerulosclerosis, a scarring pattern in the kidney's filters. It is strongly linked to APOL1. | Many AMKD patients carry an FSGS diagnosis. |
| **HIVAN** | HIVAN is kidney damage caused by HIV infection. It strikes mostly people with two APOL1 risk copies, which shows how an infection can trigger AMKD. | HIV-associated nephropathy, a kidney disease strongly linked to APOL1. | Part of the APOL1 disease spectrum. |
| **Acromegaly** | Acromegaly is a rare disorder in which the body makes too much growth hormone, which enlarges the hands, feet, and organs. Palsonify treats it. | A hormone disorder in which the body makes too much growth hormone. | Palsonify's disease. |
| **CAH** | Congenital adrenal hyperplasia is an inherited disorder of the hormones made by the adrenal glands. Atumelnant targets it. | Congenital adrenal hyperplasia, an inherited hormone disorder of the adrenal glands. | Atumelnant's disease. |
| **IgAN** | IgA nephropathy is an immune disease in which a faulty antibody lodges in the kidney and damages it. It is povetacicept's first market and the reason Vertex has kidney reps. | IgA nephropathy, an autoimmune kidney disease. | Povetacicept's first indication and the reason Vertex already has nephrology reps. |
| **pMN** | Primary membranous nephropathy is an immune disease that damages the kidney's filters. Vertex is testing povetacicept in it. | Primary membranous nephropathy, an autoimmune kidney disease. | Povetacicept's second kidney indication, in pivotal trials. |
| **ADPKD** | ADPKD is an inherited disease in which fluid-filled cysts grow in the kidneys until they fail. VX-407 targets a genetic subset of it. | Autosomal dominant polycystic kidney disease, an inherited disease in which cysts grow in the kidneys. | VX-407's target. |
| **CF** | Cystic fibrosis is an inherited disease that clogs the lungs and digestive system with thick mucus. Vertex's cystic fibrosis pills produced 98% of its 2025 revenue. | Cystic fibrosis, a genetic lung and digestive disease. | Vertex's largest business. |
| **CFTR** | CFTR is the protein that goes wrong in cystic fibrosis. Vertex's cystic fibrosis pills help the faulty protein work. | Cystic fibrosis transmembrane conductance regulator, the protein that is faulty in CF. | The target of Vertex's CF drugs. |
| **SCD** | Sickle cell disease is an inherited blood disease that bends red blood cells into a sickle shape and causes attacks of severe pain. Casgevy treats it. | Sickle cell disease. | Casgevy's indication. |
| **TDT** | Transfusion-dependent beta thalassemia is an inherited blood disease in which patients need regular blood transfusions to live. Casgevy treats it. | Transfusion-dependent beta thalassemia. | Casgevy's other indication. |
| **T1D / T2D** | Type 1 diabetes is an autoimmune disease that destroys the body's insulin-making cells; type 2 is the common form tied to weight and age. Many people with kidney disease in primary care also have type 2 diabetes. | Type 1 diabetes (autoimmune) and type 2 diabetes (metabolic). | T1D is the zimislecel target. T2D was one of the AMPLIFIED cohorts. |
| **DPN** | DPN is nerve damage caused by diabetes, usually in the feet and legs. Some patients feel burning pain, and that painful form is the use Vertex is testing for Journavx. | Diabetic peripheral neuropathy: length-dependent damage to peripheral nerves from diabetes. Painful DPN, the suzetrigine indication under study, is neuropathic pain from that damage. Vertex puts the US DPN population at about 2.5 million (WS-G, source S4). | Primary care treats much DPN, but Vertex places the program in its specialty Journavx force (WS-G). Phase 3 data are expected about mid-2027. |
| **DM1** | Myotonic dystrophy type 1 is an inherited disease that weakens the muscles. VX-670 targets it. | Myotonic dystrophy type 1, a genetic muscle disease. | VX-670's target. |
| **wAIHA** | Warm autoimmune hemolytic anemia is an immune disease in which antibodies destroy the body's own red blood cells. Vertex tested povetacicept in it. | Warm autoimmune hemolytic anemia. | A povetacicept indication in Phase 1/2. |
| **gMG** | Generalized myasthenia gravis is an immune disease that blocks nerve signals to the muscles and causes weakness. Vertex is testing povetacicept in it. | Generalized myasthenia gravis, an autoimmune neuromuscular disease. | A povetacicept indication in Phase 1/2. |
| **HCV** | HCV is the hepatitis C virus, which damages the liver. Vertex's hepatitis C drug Incivek launched fast in 2011 and then lost its market to better drugs by 2014. | Hepatitis C virus. | Incivek's market. |

## 3. Kidney tests and clinical measures

| Term | Business meaning | Technical meaning | Why it matters here |
|---|---|---|---|
| **eGFR** | eGFR is the standard blood-test measure of how well the kidneys clean the blood; about 90 or more is normal, and below 15 means kidney failure. Doctors track how fast it falls over time. | Estimated glomerular filtration rate: how well the kidneys filter, estimated from a blood test. Lower means worse. | "eGFR slope," the rate of decline over time, is an AMPLITUDE endpoint. |
| **Proteinuria** | Proteinuria means protein leaking into the urine. Healthy kidneys keep protein in the blood, so leakage is an early sign of filter damage and a measure trials use to show that a drug works. | Excess urinary protein, measured as UPCR (g/g) or UACR (mg/g); normal UACR is under 30 mg/g. AMPLITUDE admits UPCR 0.7 to under 10 g/g (Morgan Stanley p.12). AMPLIFIED's modest-proteinuria group falls below the AMPLITUDE floor (check). | AMPLITUDE's floor of 0.7 g/g sits far below the nephrotic range of about 3.5 g a day. UBS cites studies placing about 80% of AMKD patients in the modest range. |
| **UACR** | UACR is a low-cost urine test that measures how much albumin, the main blood protein, leaks into the urine. Primary care doctors order it routinely for patients with diabetes or high blood pressure. | Urine albumin-to-creatinine ratio in mg/g from a spot urine sample; dividing by creatinine corrects for urine concentration. KDIGO categories: A1 under 30, A2 30–300, A3 over 300 mg/g. | AMPLIFIED reported a 42.7% UACR cut (95% CI −58.3% to −21.1%) in modest proteinuria. A high UACR in a patient with African ancestry could prompt an APOL1 test. |
| **UPCR** | UPCR is a cheap urine test that measures how much protein leaks into the urine, a sign of damaged kidney filters. Inaxaplin's trials use it to show whether the drug works. | Urine protein-to-creatinine ratio, a similar urine protein test. | Used in AMPLIFIED results and trial entry criteria. |
| **Genotyping / genetic test** | A genetic test reads a person's genes from a blood or saliva sample. For AMKD, it shows whether someone carries two risky APOL1 copies, and it is the step primary care rarely takes. | A lab test (blood or saliva) that reads a person's APOL1 variants. | The diagnosis bottleneck; "genetic testing uplift" is a required case output. |
| **Sponsored testing** | Sponsored testing means Vertex pays labs so clinicians can order the APOL1 genetic test at no cost to the patient. The programs already run, so the case counts them in the baseline. | Manufacturer-funded genetic testing with counseling through Labcorp, Natera (Renasight panel), and Arkana. At Labcorp any clinician may order for patients with African ancestry, CKD, no diabetes, and no dialysis or transplant; results take about 2 weeks; no identifiable data go to Vertex. OIG treats a free test as remuneration under the AKS. | Genetic testing uplift counts only tests above those these programs already produce (prompt v4, trap 7). Morgan Stanley p.5: "VRTX offers free APOL1 genotyping." |
| **Nephrotic range** / **nephrotic syndrome** | Nephrotic range means very heavy protein loss in the urine. Nephrotic syndrome is that loss plus low blood protein and swelling. Patients with it are sicker and more likely to reach a kidney specialist. | Nephrotic-range proteinuria is conventionally 3.5 g or more a day in adults. Nephrotic syndrome adds low serum albumin, edema, and usually high blood lipids. Inaxaplin's Phase 2a split patients at UPCR 2.7 g/g (Morgan Stanley p.6). | Filspari's April 13, 2026 FSGS approval covers only patients without nephrotic syndrome (Travere release, via WS-H), a precedent for a narrow kidney label. |
| **SOC** | Standard of care is the treatment doctors give today by default. For AMKD it means blood-pressure drugs that protect the kidneys, because no drug targets the genetic cause. | The accepted treatment under current guidelines, used as background therapy or comparator in trials. For AMKD, SOC is ACE inhibitor or ARB therapy with blood-pressure control; AMPLITUDE tests inaxaplin against placebo on top of it (check). | Jefferies' expert calls biopsy review complementary SOC in advanced proteinuria (PDF p.79). Inaxaplin must add benefit on top of SOC. |
| **ACEi / ARB** | ACE inhibitors and ARBs are common, low-cost blood-pressure pills that also protect the kidneys. They are the standard care AMKD patients get today. | ACE inhibitors and angiotensin receptor blockers, blood pressure drugs that protect the kidneys. | The current standard of care for AMKD. |
| **RAAS** | RAAS is the hormone system that controls blood pressure and fluid balance. ACE inhibitors and ARBs work by blocking parts of it. | Renin-angiotensin-aldosterone system, the hormone system that ACEi and ARBs act on. | Background term. |
| **SGLT2i** | SGLT2 inhibitors, such as Farxiga and Jardiance, are diabetes pills that also slow kidney disease, including in people without diabetes. Their makers already promote kidney care to primary care doctors. | SGLT2 inhibitors (for example Farxiga, Jardiance), diabetes drugs that also slow CKD. | Their makers already promote kidney care to PCPs, which makes them a precedent and a competitor for PCP attention. |
| **KDIGO** | KDIGO is the international group that writes the kidney-care guidelines doctors follow. Its advice on when to test for APOL1 shapes how many patients get tested. | Kidney Disease: Improving Global Outcomes, the organization that publishes kidney care guidelines. | Guideline adoption of APOL1 testing would change testing rates. |
| **ICD-10** | ICD-10 is the system of diagnosis codes doctors put on insurance claims. A disease with its own code can be counted and found in claims data. | The standard diagnosis coding system used on medical claims. | AMKD now has specific ICD-10 codes, which make it possible to find patients in claims data and target PCPs. |
| **N07.B / Z84.11** | N07.B and Z84.11 are insurance-claim codes, in use since Oct 1, 2025, for AMKD itself and for a family history of it. ## 4. Clinical trials and regulation

| Term | Business meaning | Technical meaning | Why it matters here |
|---|---|---|---|
| **FDA** | The FDA is the US agency that decides whether a drug can be sold and what its maker may say in advertising. It will decide on inaxaplin. | US Food and Drug Administration. | It approves inaxaplin and regulates drug advertising. |
| **Phase 1 / 2 / 3** | Phases 1, 2, and 3 are the stages of human testing a new drug passes: safety in a few people, then early evidence that it works, then large trials that prove it. | **Pivotal trial** | A pivotal trial is the large study whose results the FDA uses to decide on approval. AMPLITUDE is inaxaplin's pivotal trial. | The trial whose results support approval. | AMPLITUDE is inaxaplin's pivotal trial. |
| **POC** | Proof of concept means early evidence that a drug does what it was designed to do. AMPLIFIED was a proof-of-concept study in new patient groups. | Proof of concept: early evidence that a drug works. | AMPLIFIED is a POC study. |
| **IA** | An interim analysis is a planned early look at trial results before the trial ends. | **Endpoint** | An endpoint is the measurement a trial uses to decide whether a drug worked, such as the change in kidney function. | **Placebo** | A placebo is a dummy pill that looks like the drug. | **Open-label** | An open-label study tells patients and doctors which drug is given. | **CI** (confidence interval) | A confidence interval is the range of values a study's result is consistent with. | **PRV** | A priority review voucher is a coupon FDA gives for some rare-disease approvals. | **NCE** exclusivity | New chemical entity exclusivity is 5 years in which FDA will not accept a generic copy of a new drug, whatever its patents say. | **NDA / BLA** | An NDA or a BLA is the formal application a company files to ask the FDA to approve a drug. | **PDUFA date** | The PDUFA date is the deadline by which the FDA has promised to decide on a drug application. Povetacicept's is Nov 30, 2026. | The FDA's target date to decide on an application (named for the Prescription Drug User Fee Act). | Povetacicept's is Nov 30, 2026. |
| **Label** | A drug's label is the FDA-approved statement of who the drug is for and how to use it. Sales reps and ads may discuss only the uses on the label. | The FDA-approved description of who a drug is for. | The label decides which patients count in the model. |
| **Orphan drug designation** | Orphan designation is an FDA status for drugs that treat rare diseases, meaning fewer than 200,000 US patients. | **Orange Book** / **Purple Book** | The Orange Book and Purple Book are FDA's public lists of approved drugs and their market protections. | **LOE** | Loss of exclusivity is the date when a drug's patent protection ends and cheaper copies can be sold. | **MLR** | Medical-legal-regulatory review is the internal check that every ad, brochure, and sales-rep script must pass before use. | **Unbranded vs. branded** | Branded material names the drug and is allowed only after approval. ## 6. Finance and valuation

| Term | Business meaning | Technical meaning | Why it matters here |
|---|---|---|---|
| **NPV** | Net present value adds up the money an investment will bring in and pay out over the years, counts future dollars as worth less than today's, and subtracts the cost. A value above zero means the investment creates value. | Net present value: future cash flows converted to today's dollars at a discount rate, minus the investment. Positive means the investment creates value. | A required case output. |
| **rNPV** | Risk-adjusted NPV multiplies the value of an investment by the chance that the drug is approved and works. It matters because inaxaplin is not yet approved. | Risk-adjusted NPV: cash flows multiplied by the probability that the drug is approved and succeeds. | Useful because inaxaplin is not yet approved. |
| **Pull-forward** | A pull-forward patient is someone a program diagnoses earlier who would have been diagnosed later anyway. The program earns credit only for the extra years of treatment, not for the whole patient. | A patient diagnosed earlier because of the program who would have been diagnosed later anyway. | The model credits only the added treated years for these patients, not their full lifetime revenue. |
| **Response curve** | A response curve shows how results grow as spending grows; each added rep or dollar usually adds less than the one before. It answers why a plan uses this many reps and not more. | How added prescriptions change as headcount or budget rises; each added rep or dollar usually adds less than the one before. | Used to choose headcount and budget, and to answer "why this many reps?" |
| **ROI** | Return on investment compares what an investment earns with what it costs. The case asks for it, and the team must state exactly how it is calculated. | Return on investment: gain divided by cost. The team must state which version it uses. | A required case output. |
| **Payback period** | The payback period is how long it takes for an investment's returns to add up to its cost. The case asks for it. | How long until cumulative cash flow turns positive. It can be simple or discounted. | A required case output. |
| **IRR** | The internal rate of return is the yearly return an investment earns. An investment adds value when its internal rate of return exceeds the company's cost of money. | Internal rate of return: the discount rate at which NPV equals zero. | An optional extra metric. |
| **WACC** | WACC is the average return Vertex's investors expect, used as the interest rate that converts future cash into today's dollars. A higher rate makes distant launch profits worth less today. | Weighted average cost of capital = E/V × cost of equity + D/V × after-tax cost of debt. A single project's rate should reflect that project's risk, not the whole firm's. | The seven sponsor reports use 7.5% (Jefferies) to 10% (Morgan Stanley), not 8–10% (decision log D-004). Each is a whole-company DCF rate. |
| **DCF** | A discounted cash flow valuation adds up a company's or project's expected future cash, converted into today's dollars. Analysts use it to set share price targets. | Value = Σ free cash flow in year t ÷ (1 + r)^t + terminal value ÷ (1 + r)^N, with r usually the WACC. Enterprise value plus net cash gives equity value; dividing by diluted shares gives value per share. | No sponsor report gives an inaxaplin-specific discount rate; all seven use whole-company DCF rates of 7.5–10% (analyst_six_summary.md, note 4). |
| **TG** (terminal growth) | Terminal growth is the yearly growth rate an analyst assumes for a company's cash after the detailed forecast ends. It covers every later year, so small changes move the valuation by hundreds of dollars per share. | Terminal value = free cash flow in year N+1 ÷ (r − g) under the Gordon growth formula, where g is the terminal (perpetuity or residual) growth rate. The sponsor reports use −1% (Oppenheimer) to 2.5% (RBC). | Oppenheimer's DCF grid runs from $438 to $880 per share across 6.5–9.0% discount rates and −4% to +2% growth (p.20). |
| **SOTP** | Sum-of-the-parts valuation values each business or drug separately and adds the pieces. It shows how much of a share price analysts assign to one product. | Total equity value = Σ probability-adjusted NPV of each product or unit + net cash. Jefferies' bars sum to $580: $48 net cash, $376 CF, $81 povetacicept, $25 pain, $30 kidney pipeline, and $20 other (PDF p.17). | Jefferies' $30 per share for inaxaplin plus VX-407 equals about $7.68B on 256M shares (calculated), a check on the model's inaxaplin value. |
| **EPS** | Earnings per share is the company's profit divided by its number of shares. Investors watch it every quarter, so new spending that lowers near-term profit draws attention. | EPS = net income ÷ weighted average shares; diluted EPS adds options and convertible securities. Analysts usually quote non-GAAP EPS. | Truist has non-GAAP EPS of $18.40 (2025A), $19.43 (2026E), and $21.77 (2027E) (p.5). A $50M program costs about $0.20 per share before tax ($50M ÷ 256M shares). |
| **P/E** | The price-to-earnings ratio is the share price divided by yearly earnings per share. It shows how many years of current profit investors pay for a share. | P/E = price per share ÷ EPS, on trailing or forward earnings. Multiples are written with an x, for example 26.2x. | William Blair shows P/E of 29.7x (2025A), 26.2x (2026E), and 27.7x (2027E) (p.1); Morgan Stanley shows 24.5x to 18.3x for 2025–2028 (p.1). |
| **GAAP** / **non-GAAP** | GAAP figures follow standard US accounting rules. Non-GAAP figures are the company's own adjusted version that leaves out items such as stock pay and deal costs, so the two can differ by billions of dollars in a deal year. | Generally Accepted Accounting Principles. Non-GAAP measures exclude items such as stock-based compensation, acquired IPR&D, and amortization; SEC rules require a reconciliation to the nearest GAAP measure. | Vertex's Q2 2026 non-GAAP SG&A was $520.2M, 44.7% above Q2 2025 (WS-G). | **IPR&D** | Acquired in-process R&D is the part of a purchase price paid for drugs still in testing. | **PoS / PTRS** (also written **POS**) | Probability of success is an analyst's estimate of the chance that a drug in testing reaches approval.
