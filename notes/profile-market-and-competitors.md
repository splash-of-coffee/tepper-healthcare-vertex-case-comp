# Market and Competitor Profile: AMKD, Inaxaplin, and Vertex's Business Lines

Written Sept 27, 2026 for the Tepper Healthcare Case Competition team. This file covers the market for APOL1-mediated kidney disease (AMKD), the drugs that compete with inaxaplin, the companies that already promote kidney care in primary care, and Vertex's competitors in its other business lines. The last part states what these facts mean for each sales and advertising option.

**How to read the evidence**
- Numbers in square brackets, such as [4], point to the numbered Sources list at the end. Analyst figures carry the sponsor report's page as the team's extraction files record it.
- Confidence ratings: **high** means a company release, SEC filing, FDA or CMS document, or trial registry read directly. **Medium** means a reputable news outlet, a secondary data site, or a sponsor analyst report. **Low** means a machine-written summary or a figure the team could not trace.
- "Inference" marks a conclusion the team drew that no source states. "Team projection" marks the team's own date or share estimate.
- The session's web-search allowance ran out partway through the research. The later checks used direct reads of company, FDA, CMS, SEC, and trial-registry pages. The Open questions section lists what the team could not check.

## Summary

- **Maze Therapeutics' MZE829 is the only other APOL1 drug with patient data.** It cut urine albumin (a protein) by 35.6% on average in 12 patients after 12 weeks, and by 48.6% in patients without diabetes [2]. Maze plans a pivotal trial (the trial meant to support approval) in the first half of 2027 and held $494.9M in cash on June 30, 2026 [1]. The team projects MZE829's earliest US approval in Nov 2030 and a base date of Nov 2031 (team projection). Inaxaplin would then sell alone for about 3.7 years after a base approval in Mar 2028 (team projection).
- **The other APOL1 programs trail Maze.** AstraZeneca's antisense drug AZD2373 stopped enrolling at 57 patients, and its Phase 2b trial ends on Oct 4, 2027 [4]. A Duke trial of baricitinib, supported by Eli Lilly, closed with 8 patients and no posted results [5]. The team found no APOL1 program sponsored in China [8, 16].
- **The four sponsor reports put inaxaplin's 2035 risk-adjusted sales between $1.36B (RBC) and $2.73B (Jefferies)** [18–21]. Truist sizes the primary-AMKD market at "~$5B+" [21].
- **Makers of diabetes drugs with kidney labels already reach primary care at scale.** Medicare Part D covered 1,883,000 Jardiance users in 2023 and 2,282,000 semaglutide users in 2024 [34, 35]. No public source the team found measures these companies' share of primary care physicians' attention.
- **Povetacicept would be the seventh branded US drug for IgA nephropathy if the FDA approves it on Nov 30, 2026** [26, 53]. The nephrology reps will therefore have little spare time for primary care calls in 2027 (inference).

## Terms used in this file

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| AMKD | APOL1-mediated kidney disease: kidney disease in people who carry two high-risk copies (G1 or G2) of the APOL1 gene. |
| Primary AMKD | AMKD without diabetes or another known cause of kidney disease. It is the AMPLITUDE trial population and the likely first inaxaplin label. |
| UACR / UPCR | Urine albumin-to-creatinine ratio and urine protein-to-creatinine ratio: urine tests that measure protein leaking through the kidney's filters. |
| eGFR | Estimated glomerular filtration rate: kidney function estimated from a blood test. |
| FSGS | Focal segmental glomerulosclerosis: scarring of the kidney's filters seen on biopsy. APOL1 causes some FSGS. |
| CKD | Chronic kidney disease. |
| Pivotal trial | The trial whose results support approval. |
| Accelerated approval | FDA approval based on an early marker such as proteinuria or one-year eGFR, with confirmatory data due later. |
| Risk-adjusted sales | Forecast sales multiplied by the analyst's probability that the drug is approved. |
| Antisense drug | A short synthetic strand of genetic material that stops cells from making a target protein. |
| JAK inhibitor | A pill that blocks JAK enzymes, which carry immune signals. Baricitinib is one. |
| SGLT2 inhibitor | A diabetes pill that makes the kidney pass sugar into the urine. It also slows CKD. Farxiga and Jardiance are SGLT2 inhibitors. |
| GLP-1 receptor agonist | A drug that copies a gut hormone to lower blood sugar and weight. Semaglutide (Ozempic) is one. |
| Non-steroidal MRA | A drug that blocks the receptor for the hormone aldosterone, which drives kidney and heart scarring. Finerenone (Kerendia) is one. |
| IgAN | IgA nephropathy, an autoimmune kidney disease. It is povetacicept's first indication. |
| BAFF / APRIL | Immune signaling proteins that drive the abnormal antibody in IgAN. |
| Medicare Part D | Medicare's outpatient prescription drug benefit. |
| Negotiated price | The Medicare Part D price that CMS set for a drug under the Inflation Reduction Act (the statute calls it the "maximum fair price"). |
| HEDIS | Healthcare Effectiveness Data and Information Set: quality measures that health plans report to NCQA (the National Committee for Quality Assurance). |
| Loss of exclusivity | The point at which generic copies can enter the market. |
| Unbranded advertising | Disease-awareness advertising that names no drug. |

**How complete the search was.** The ClinicalTrials.gov registry lists 20 interventional studies that mention APOL1 [8]. Nine of them test an APOL1-directed drug, and they cover the five drug programs in the table that have reached people. A 2014 Acthar study in APOL1 kidney disease was withdrawn with no patients [8]. None of the 20 studies has a sponsor in China. A Chinese-language web search and a Feb 28, 2026 Chinese review of APOL1 treatment named no Chinese developer [16]. The team did not search China's own trial registry (ChiCTR).

**A competitor's own genotype test.** Almac Diagnostic Services registered a study to genotype about 2,000 candidates for an unnamed company's Phase 2b antisense trial in AMKD, with completion due Feb 2027 [7]. The trial it describes matches AstraZeneca's AZD2373 study (inference). A competitor with its own validated APOL1 test would not depend on the labs Vertex pays for testing (inference).

**How investors read the Maze data.** Maze shares fell about 38% on Mar 25, 2026, the day of the HORIZON results [11]. Mizuho wrote that "There is a drug here that is likely approvable (and commercial)" [11]. Maze shares rose 26.75% on Sept 22, 2026, from $22.39 to $28.38, after Vertex's AMPLIFIED results, and closed at $26.05 on Sept 25 [9]. Truist wrote that the market could support "blockbuster sales" for MZE829 even if it only matches inaxaplin [12, 22]. Jefferies wrote in March that it is "not worried" about MZE829 (PDF p.76) [18]. Stifel wrote that MZE829 showed little response in diabetic AMKD (paraphrase in an AI-assisted summary) [24].

**Maze's stated plans.** Maze said on Sept 9, 2026 that its base case is a one-year endpoint for accelerated approval, "similar to Vertex's trial approach," and that it may start with non-diabetic AMKD [13]. That source is an AI-assisted summary (low confidence), and it misdates Maze's $495M of cash to Q3 2026; Maze's own release dates $494.9M to June 30, 2026 [1, 13].

**Orphan exclusivity would not block Maze.** US orphan-drug exclusivity blocks only the same active molecule, so it would not delay MZE829 even if inaxaplin earned it [64].

Inaxaplin's time alone on the market runs from Mar 2028 to Nov 2031 in the base case, about 3.7 years (team projection). The range runs from about 2.4 years (Jun 2028 to Nov 2030) to about 5 years (Oct 2027 to Nov 2032) (team projection). Two facts could shift these dates. Enrollment in Maze's placebo-controlled trial may slow once US patients can get an approved APOL1 drug (inference). Maze's cash runs "into 2029," before its projected launch, so Maze will likely need new capital or a partner before it can sell MZE829 (inference from [1]).

**Share Vertex might keep (team projection).** No source measures how a second APOL1 drug would split patients. The table applies explicit assumptions. New patient starts rise in a straight line from 2028 to 2035, weighted 1 through 8 (total 36). Each patient stays on the first APOL1 drug for life, as Jefferies expects for inaxaplin (PDF p.81) [18]. MZE829 takes a fixed share of new starts from its first full year on the market.

| Scenario | MZE829 first full year | MZE829 share of new starts (assumption) | Calculation of Maze's share of patients in 2035 | Vertex share of US APOL1-drug patients in 2035 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Strong competitor | 2031 | 50% | 0.50 × (4+5+6+7+8) ÷ 36 = 41.7% | 58% |
| Base | 2032 | 35% | 0.35 × (5+6+7+8) ÷ 36 = 25.3% | 75% |
| Weak competitor | 2033 | 25% | 0.25 × (6+7+8) ÷ 36 = 14.6% | 85% |

The model ignores patients who stop treatment, so it overstates the value of Vertex's early patients (inference). A Jefferies expert expects Maze "to mirror VRTX access strategy in-market" (PDF p.79), so payers would likely apply the same genotype, biopsy, and disease-activity rules to both drugs (inference) [18]. Neither drug has shown a strong effect in diabetic AMKD so far [2, 25].

**Vertex's testing spend also helps the later competitor (inference).**
- A person's APOL1 genotype never changes, so each positive test identifies a patient for life (inference).
- Vertex's sponsored Labcorp testing program shares no identifiable patient or provider data with Vertex [42]. Vertex therefore gains no private patient list from the testing it pays for (inference).
- The AMKD diagnosis code N07.B took effect Oct 1, 2025 (MS p.5) [19]. Diagnosed patients appear in claims data, where any company can find the physicians who treat them (inference).
- Unbranded ads name no drug, so they raise testing for every APOL1 drug (inference).
- In the base case, patients diagnosed from 2032 onward split 65% to inaxaplin and 35% to MZE829, while patients diagnosed before late 2031 have only inaxaplin (team projection). A testing dollar spent after MZE829's launch therefore yields 35% fewer inaxaplin patients than the same dollar spent before it (team projection).
- Maze's and AstraZeneca's trials also genotype patients and raise physician awareness, which helps Vertex (inference from [7]).

**Background therapy stays in place.** HORIZON required at least 8 weeks of stable background CKD therapy, including SGLT2 inhibitors and GLP-1 drugs [2]. Inaxaplin is therefore likely to be added to these drugs rather than replace them (inference).

**Finerenone in non-diabetic kidney disease.** FIND-CKD tested finerenone against placebo on eGFR slope over 32 months [32]. The trial ended Feb 2, 2026, and the registry showed no results at its last update on Feb 13, 2026 [32]. Kerendia's label still covers CKD only with type 2 diabetes [30]. The team could not find the FIND-CKD result (open question).

**Sparsentan's 2026 FDA review.** The FDA extended the FSGS review by 3 months, from Jan 13 to Apr 13, 2026, after a major amendment [33]. The FDA then approved Filspari "to reduce proteinuria in adult and pediatric patients aged 8 years and older with focal segmental glomerulosclerosis (FSGS) without nephrotic syndrome" [33]. In the DUPLEX trial's non-nephrotic group, proteinuria fell 48% on sparsentan against 27% on irbesartan (p = 0.0075), and eGFR differed by 1.1 mL/min/1.73 m² [33]. Travere puts this US population at more than 30,000 people [33]. Travere calls Filspari "the First and Only Approved Medicine for FSGS" [33].

## Part E. What the market means for each option

**Hiring a primary care force (Option 1a).** A hired force gains from inaxaplin's time alone on the market, which the team projects at about 3.7 years in the base case (team projection). A hired force needs years to repay its fixed cost, so a longer solo period helps it more than it helps a contract force (inference). The competitor facts also hurt a hired force. MZE829's projected launch in Nov 2030 to Nov 2032 falls inside a hired force's first 5 years, and the base case then sends 35% of newly diagnosed patients to Maze (team projection). The force would carry one product into offices where Jardiance and semaglutide already reach 1.9 million and 2.3 million Part D patients (inference from [34, 35]). Falling prices for those drugs may reduce their promotion by 2028, which would help a new entrant (inference). The market facts, taken together, weaken a permanent hire made before Vertex knows MZE829's pivotal trial design and timing (inference).

**Contracting a primary care force (Option 1b).** A contracted force matches the competitor timeline best among the three sales choices (inference). Vertex can set the contract term to end near MZE829's projected launch, then renew or shrink it (inference). A testing-and-referral call made before MZE829 launches yields inaxaplin patients at the full rate, while the same call after launch yields 35% fewer in the base case (team projection). The facts that hurt a contracted force hurt every sales option. Its promotion raises APOL1 testing that Maze can later use, and its reps compete for office time with SGLT2 and GLP-1 reps (inference).

**Retargeting the nephrology reps to top primary care targets (Option 1c).** The IgAN market hurts retargeting. Six branded IgAN drugs already hold US approval, and povetacicept would be the seventh [26, 53]. Voyxact reached the market on Nov 25, 2025, and Trutakna on Jul 7, 2026, so povetacicept's first year runs against two new same-pathway rivals [53]. Nephrologist calls moved to primary care in 2027 would come out of that contested launch (inference). The Filspari FSGS approval of Apr 13, 2026 helps retargeting slightly [33]. The same reps already call on the nephrologists who receive AMKD referrals and can now treat APOL1-FSGS with an approved drug (inference). The competitor timeline does not favor retargeting either way, because MZE829's projected launch comes after povetacicept's main launch years (inference).

**Adding direct-to-patient testing ads (Option 2).** The competitor facts cut both ways for added testing ads. Unbranded ads name no drug, so each test they prompt also serves any later APOL1 drug (inference). A genotype result lasts for life, the N07.B code puts diagnosed patients into claims data, and Vertex's sponsored lab shares no patient or provider data with Vertex [19, 42]. Vertex therefore builds no private patient list with its ad spending (inference). The timing favors spending early. The team projects no second APOL1 drug before Nov 2030, so patients the ads find in 2027–2030 would start on inaxaplin, and lifelong therapy would keep most of them there (team projection; Jefferies PDF p.81 [18]). The diabetic population is a risk for broad ads. Maze counts 40% of US AMKD patients as diabetic (MS p.5), and inaxaplin cut UACR by 17.3% in diabetic AMKD with an interval that includes no effect [19, 25]. Ads that reach diabetic patients through primary care would find many APOL1-positive patients outside inaxaplin's likely first label (inference). Front-loaded, unbranded testing ads aimed at non-diabetic patients with kidney disease fit these facts best (inference).
