# Vertex Pharmaceuticals — Company Profile (as of Sept 27, 2026)

This profile gives a teammate the Vertex facts a judge from Vertex will expect the team to know. It draws on the research streams in `work/research/` and cites the original source for each fact; source numbers in brackets refer to the list at the end. Items marked "inference" are the team's conclusions, not facts from a source.

## Summary

- Vertex earned $12.0B of revenue in 2025, and cystic fibrosis (CF) drugs produced 98.3% of it. Its Aug 3, 2026 guidance is $13.1–13.2B for 2026, with at least $0.5B from drugs outside CF [1][2].
- Vertex says it works on "serious diseases" where it understands "causal human biology" [3], and it sells through what it calls a "specialty commercial model" [4]. It has no primary care sales force in any of its five business lines (case p.6; [5]).
- Kidney disease is Vertex's next business line. The FDA decides on povetacicept for IgA nephropathy by Nov 30, 2026 [6]; inaxaplin's interim analysis comes in early 2027 [7]; management said the kidney business "will ultimately rival the scale of our CF business" (Feb 12, 2026, quoted in Jefferies, Mar 10, 2026, PDF p.85).
- Vertex has built a broad field force fast once. Its Journavx pain force doubled to 300 reps in one quarter of 2026, "slightly ahead of plan" [8].
- The CEO, Reshma Kewalramani, is a nephrologist [9]. Vertex's own history includes a broad launch that a better drug displaced: Incivek for hepatitis C (2011–2014) [10].

## 2. Strategy, in Vertex's own words

- **Founding mission.** Vertex set out to "transform the way serious diseases are treated," using structure-based drug design: designing molecules to fit the three-dimensional shape of a disease protein [12].
- **Selection rule today.** The CEO describes the focus as "diseases with high unmet need, where we understand the causal human biology," with medicines that "either transform the disease or cure the disease" [3]. The rule selects diseases by the biology, not by patient count, which is why Vertex works on rare CF and on common acute pain alike (inference).
- **How it sells.** On the Crinetics deal, the CEO said the acquired drugs treat patients seen by "a concentrated group of specialized endocrinologists," which "fits directly within Vertex's proven efficient specialty commercial model" [4].
- **Primary care.** None of Vertex's six earnings calls from Q1 2025 to Q2 2026 used the phrase "primary care" (WS-G keyword search of the transcripts). An investor-relations remark in September 2026 said a move into musculoskeletal pain "could require a commercial partner because it would involve a primary-care market" [14] (an automated summary; medium-low confidence). The deck should not credit Vertex with a primary care plan it has not announced.
- **Kidney ambition.** "We anticipate that the renal franchise will ultimately rival the scale of our CF business" (Feb 12, 2026; Jefferies PDF p.85).

**What the concentration means.** The risk is concentration in one disease, not near-term patent loss. Trikafta's compound patents run to December 2037 and Alyftrek's to February 2039 (FDA Orange Book) [15]. The older CF drugs lose protection sooner: Symdeko's basic patent expires in 2027 and Kalydeco's, with pediatric exclusivity, in 2028; Lupin's generic ivacaftor has tentative FDA approval [5][15]. Those three drugs produced $644.7M, 5.4% of 2025 revenue [2].

**Consumer marketing.** Vertex runs a Journavx connected-TV (streaming TV) pilot that started in January 2026 and a telehealth site, Informed Pain Care, linked from journavx.com [8][19].

**Programs Vertex already runs for AMKD (the case's baseline includes all of them):**
- **Free APOL1 genetic testing** with genetic counseling through three labs [20]:
  - Labcorp: any clinician may order; eligibility is African ancestry, chronic kidney disease, no diabetes, and no dialysis or transplant; results in about 2 weeks; no identifiable data goes to Vertex.
  - Natera: a kidney gene panel that includes APOL1.
  - Arkana: eligibility includes Hispanic or Latino patients.
- **Power Forward**, an unbranded patient-awareness campaign with basketball Hall of Famer Alonzo Mourning, running since Nov 4, 2022 [21].
- **An American Kidney Fund APOL1 awareness campaign** that Vertex funds [22].
- **Physician education sites**: apol1ckd.com and the vrtxmedical.com AMKD pages [23].
- **Diagnosis codes** for AMKD (N07.B) and family history of AMKD (Z84.11) took effect Oct 1, 2025 (Morgan Stanley p.5) [24]. Vertex's own materials promoted them to nephrologists.

## 8. The kidney business

- **Povetacicept** blocks two immune signals (BAFF and APRIL) that drive IgA nephropathy. If approved, it would be the seventh branded US drug for IgA nephropathy and the third acting on the APRIL pathway approved in 12 months, after Otsuka's Voyxact (Nov 25, 2025) and Vera's Trutakna (Jul 7, 2026) [25]; see `notes/profile-market-and-competitors.md`, Part D4. It went from interim readout (Mar 10, 2026) to FDA decision date (Nov 30, 2026) in 8.7 months, using a rolling filing and a priority review voucher (WS-G section 4).
- **Inaxaplin** could be Vertex's second kidney launch. The CEO said on Aug 3, 2026 that the FDA agreed accelerated approval can rest on one-year kidney function (eGFR) at the interim analysis [4]. An FDA decision in Q4 2027 is the earliest plausible date if Vertex repeats the povetacicept timeline (team projection, WS-G).
- **The same nephrology reps** will carry all three kidney drugs, because Vertex built the force "with the breadth of our renal pipeline in mind" [4]. Their calls in 2027 go first to the povetacicept launch (inference).

## 9. Deals and capital allocation

Vertex paid about $16.4B in headline value for five acquisitions since 2019: Crinetics ($10.0B), Alpine ($4.9B), Semma ($0.95B), ViaCyte ($0.32B), and Exonics ($0.245B upfront) (WS-G section 6). Every acquired drug treats a disease that specialists manage (inference). A $50M-a-year primary care program would equal 2.6% of Vertex's annualized 2026 selling, general, and administrative spending of about $1.9B (WS-G section 2), so cash does not limit the choice; management attention during the Crinetics integration is the scarcer resource (inference).

- Inaxaplin: 2035 risk-adjusted sales estimates in the sponsor reports run from $1.36B (RBC) to $2.73B (Jefferies). ## 12. What this profile means for the case

These are inferences for the team to test, not conclusions:
- Vertex has never run a primary care sales force, and every acquisition since 2019 is specialist-treated, so a hired primary care team (Option 1a) would be a new capability, not an extension of an existing one.
- The kidney reps are committed to the povetacicept launch in 2027, so pulling their calls toward primary care (Option 1c) competes with Vertex's first kidney launch.
- Vertex already pays for APOL1 testing and runs Power Forward, so any new spending must raise testing above what those programs produce today. The deck must present them as Vertex's existing programs, never as new ideas.
- Vertex's Incivek history and its partner-first language on primary care markets both favor commitments that can grow or shrink with the trial results.
