## Claim 1 — "The CEO is a nephrologist"

**Verdict: CONFIRMED.**

- Reshma Kewalramani, M.D., FASN, has been CEO since 2020. She was the first woman to lead a large public biotech company. She joined Vertex in 2017 as Chief Medical Officer.
- She trained in internal medicine at Massachusetts General Hospital and completed a nephrology fellowship in the MGH–Brigham and Women's program. She worked as a transplant nephrologist at Boston-area hospitals, then led R&D work at Amgen.
- She is a Fellow of the American Society of Nephrology (FASN) and served on the board of ASN's Kidney Health Initiative.
- Named a TIME Woman of the Year in 2026.
- Sources: [Vertex leadership page](https://www.vrtx.com/our-company/leadership/reshma-kewalramani/), [BU Bostonia 2026 profile](https://www.bu.edu/articles/2026/vertex-ceo-and-president-reshma-kewalramani/), [ASN Kidney News](https://www.kidneynews.org/display/post/research-10/dr.-reshma-kewalramani-named-incoming-president-and-ceo-of-vertex-pharmaceuticals--former-member-asn-khi-board-of-directors.xml), [TIME](https://time.com/collections/women-of-the-year/7377894/reshma-kewalramani-2026/).

## Claim 3 — "The disease is predominantly in African American individuals and affects ~250K people in the US"

**Verdict: ancestry CONFIRMED; ~250K matches the case figures but needs framing care.**

- **Ancestry:** AMKD requires two APOL1 high-risk variants, which are found almost entirely in people of recent African ancestry. About 13% of African Americans (about 6 million people) carry two high-risk alleles ([Kidney Medicine review, 2025](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12361766/)).
- **~250K (corrected 2026-09-27 after the audit):** the case's Vertex slide gives ~150K patients for primary AMKD (AMPLITUDE population) plus ~100K more (AMPLIFIED populations), which totals ~250K. **These figures cover the US and Europe together, not the US alone** (Morgan Stanley p.1 and p.5; Jefferies PDF p.14; Vertex's Sept 22, 2026 release: "approximately 150,000 people in the U.S. and Europe"). The US-only number is smaller and must be estimated. Maze, the competitor, estimates at least 250K **US** AMKD patients, 40% with diabetes (Morgan Stanley p.5), and says AMKD affects more than 1 million people in the US. Each source uses a different definition, so list them side by side, each with its definition.
- **An arithmetic tension the team should understand before judges ask about it:**
  - Case figures: 13% of African Americans have two variants, and 20% of those develop AMKD.
  - US Black population is about 47M, counting "alone or in combination" (2020 Census; the agent should verify).
  - Derived: 47M × 13% ≈ 6.1M with two variants; × 20% ≈ **1.2M expected to develop kidney disease over a lifetime**.
  - The 1.2M figure is a US lifetime number; Vertex's 250K is a current US-plus-Europe number. The two cannot be compared as a ratio, because they differ in both geography and time frame. The US gap between lifetime risk and today's treatable patients is larger than the raw numbers suggest.
  - The funnel must keep lifetime risk, current US prevalence, and the US label-eligible population as separate numbers, and never mix US with US-plus-Europe figures. A nephrologist-CEO audience will catch the mix.
  - The likely first label excludes people with diabetes (AMPLITUDE exclusion criteria, Jefferies PDF p.71). Many Black patients with CKD in primary care also have diabetes, so the label-eligible share of primary care patients is smaller than the total.
- **Diagnosis codes:** AMKD has its own ICD-10 codes, N07.B (AMKD) and Z84.11 (family history of AMKD), effective Oct 1, 2025 (Morgan Stanley p.5; [Vertex story](https://www.vrtx.com/stories/attention-nephrologists-new-diagnostic-codes-apol1-mediated-kidney-disease/)). N07.B finds only patients already diagnosed. Z84.11 supports family (cascade) testing.

## Claim 4 — "Vertex's bread and butter is its monopolistic CF drug, likely nearing the end of its patent life"

**Verdict: CF dominance CONFIRMED; "nearing patent expiry" is INCORRECT on current dates.**

- **Revenue:** Vertex's full-year 2025 revenue was $12.0B, up 9% year over year. The first 2026 guidance was $12.95–13.1B; on Aug 3, 2026, Vertex raised it to **$13.1–13.2B**, with non-CF products still at "$500 million or more" ([Q2 2026 release](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/875320/000087532026000256/ex-991_q22026.htm)). Derived: CF is about 96% of 2026 guided revenue, since about $0.5B non-CF out of about $13.15B total leaves about 96% for CF. Vertex will update guidance to include Crinetics.
- **Market position:** Vertex's CFTR modulators (Trikafta/Kaftrio, Alyftrek, Symdeko, Orkambi, Kalydeco) have no approved CFTR-modulator competitor. "Monopolistic" is accurate in practice.
- **Patents:** Trikafta's protection is reported to run to **2037**. Alyftrek, the next-generation once-daily triple combination approved in late 2024, runs to **2039**, with generic entry expected 2039–2041 ([GeneOnline](https://www.geneonline.com/vertex-pharmaceuticals-secures-european-approval-for-alyftrek-as-trikafta-patent-expiry-looms/)). The agent should verify both against Vertex's 10-K.
  - The CF cliff is roughly 11–13 years away, not near. Vertex is also switching patients to Alyftrek, which extends the franchise.
- **Reframe for the deck:** the risk is **revenue concentration** (about 96% from one disease), not near-term patent expiry. A judge can check the 96% figure against Vertex's own guidance, which makes it hard to dispute. It explains why diversification (kidney, pain, sickle cell, T1D) matters now.
- **Grassroots buy-in:** the Cystic Fibrosis Foundation funded Vertex's CF research through "venture philanthropy." The commonly cited figure is about $150M, and the Foundation later sold its royalty rights for about $3.3B (2014). *The agent must verify these figures before use.*
  - **Inference:** Vertex has a proven model of partnering with a patient community to accelerate a genetic disease program. No foundation funds AMKD research at CF Foundation scale, **but Vertex already partners in AMKD:** it funds an American Kidney Fund APOL1 awareness campaign, runs the unbranded "Power Forward" campaign with Alonzo Mourning (since Nov 2022), and pays for free APOL1 testing through Labcorp, Natera, and Arkana. Any partnership idea for the direct-to-patient option must build on these programs, not present them as new. Further partners to consider include the National Kidney Foundation, Black church and barbershop health networks, HBCU medical schools, and the National Medical Association.

## Claim 5 — "Vertex used to be a small biotech, now mid-size"

**Verdict: PARTLY CORRECT; the scale label needs adjusting.**

- Vertex's revenue is $12.0B (FY2025) and its market capitalization has been above $100B since late 2024 (reported at ~$122.6B during 2025). By market cap it is a **large-cap biotech**, one of the largest US biotechs.
- By revenue it is still much smaller than big pharma companies with $40–60B+ in revenue. Its **commercial footprint** is specialist-only: it has no primary care field force in any disease, per the case.
- **Suggested phrasing:** "a large-cap biotech with a specialist-sized commercial footprint." This captures what the team means, and it is the specific capability gap the case asks about.

## Claim 6 — "Initial core focus and mission was edge-case/rare diseases; CF made them blow up; now a mixed identity"

**Verdict: the history is DIFFERENT from what the team assumed. The corrected version is more useful.**

- Vertex was founded in 1989 by Joshua Boger (from Merck) and Kevin Kinsella. Its mission was to "transform the way serious diseases are treated" using **structure-based rational drug design**, and it was one of the first biotechs built on that approach.
- Its early products targeted **large infectious-disease markets**, not rare diseases: amprenavir (HIV protease inhibitor, partnered) and **telaprevir/Incivek** for hepatitis C (approved 2011).
- **Incivek** was reported as the fastest drug launch in history at the time, reaching about $1.5B in sales quickly. Sales then fell as patients waited for all-oral regimens. Vertex announced about 370 job cuts (about 15% of its workforce) in October 2013, before Gilead's Sovaldi was approved in December 2013. Incivek sales fell 96% year over year by H1 2014, and Vertex exited hepatitis C in August 2014 ([BioPharma Dive](https://www.biopharmadive.com/news/falling-sales-sovaldi-force-vertex-to-ditch-hep-c-drug/297303/), [Fierce Pharma](https://www.fiercepharma.com/sales-and-marketing/sovaldi-forces-incivek-off-hep-c-market-as-vertex-calls-it-quits)).
- The rare-disease identity came **after** that, with Kalydeco (CF, 2012) and the CFTR franchise.
- **Inference (a candidate for the deck and for Q&A):** Vertex has lived through a broad-market launch that a better competitor erased within about two years, followed by layoffs. That history affects:
  - **1a vs. 1b:** a permanent FTE primary care force is hard to unwind if a competitor (Maze's MZE829) or a narrow label shrinks the opportunity. A contracted force can be scaled down.
  - **First-mover urgency:** building diagnosis infrastructure early locks in referral relationships before a competitor arrives, but part of that market-building benefit may also flow to the competitor.
  - Test this against evidence, then decide whether it belongs on a slide or stays in the Q&A bank. A judge from Vertex will know this history.
- **"Mixed identity" today:** Vertex describes its strategy as "serious diseases" where it understands "causal human biology," regardless of population size. That already covers both rare diseases (CF, sickle cell) and large ones (Journavx for acute pain, suzetrigine for DPN, T1D). So the identity is less mixed than it looks: Vertex selects by **causal biology**, not by population size. AMKD fits that filter exactly: a genetic cause, a large underdiagnosed population, and a population concentrated in one ancestry group.

## Claim 7 — "Vertex is at an inflection point in trajectory for drugs, R&D, and target markets"

**Verdict: SUPPORTED by evidence.**

- Vertex has three recent launches outside or alongside its CF base: Casgevy (sickle cell/TDT gene editing, late 2023), Journavx (suzetrigine, non-opioid acute pain, January 2025), and Alyftrek (CF, late 2024). Non-CF revenue is guided to reach $500M+ in 2026.
- **Crinetics (added after the audit):** Vertex agreed on July 6, 2026 to buy Crinetics Pharmaceuticals for about $10.0B and closed the deal on Sept 1, 2026. The deal adds Palsonify (acromegaly, on the market) and atumelnant (congenital adrenal hyperplasia, Phase 3), which Vertex says could exceed $5B in combined peak revenue. Rare endocrine disease becomes Vertex's fifth business line. The same announcement named new leaders: Charles Wagner as COO over the integration, Jonathan Poole as CFO from Jan 1, 2027, and Jasper van Grunsven as Chief Pain and New Product Planning Officer.
- Its first kidney launch is imminent: the povetacicept PDUFA date is **November 30, 2026**.
- Inaxaplin: Vertex reported AMPLIFIED Phase 2b results on Sept 22, 2026. The modest-proteinuria result was positive; the diabetes result's confidence interval includes no effect. The AMPLITUDE interim analysis is due early 2027, with a possible accelerated-approval filing after it.
- Investor commentary is questioning "the cost of growing beyond cystic fibrosis" ([Seeking Alpha headline](https://seekingalpha.com/article/4857930-vertex-pharmaceuticals-the-cost-of-growing-beyond-cystic-fibrosis-rating-downgrade); the article itself was not read).
- **Inference for the deck:** Vertex is deciding whether to become a company that reaches patients through primary care. The AMKD decision is effectively the first test of that capability. Suzetrigine in DPN (pivotal stage) is a primary-care-heavy indication, so a primary care capability built for inaxaplin could carry over. This is a strategic option value argument for Option 1a. It must be weighed against the Incivek lesson in Claim 6, which favors flexibility. This is the central tension of the recommendation.
- **Journavx field force (verified after the audit):** it grew from about 100 reps at launch to about 150, then doubled in 2026, calling on emergency, orthopedic, anesthesia, obstetric, dental, and plastic-surgery clinicians (Cantor conference, Sept 10, 2026). Vertex has built a broad field force quickly, but not a primary care force for a chronic disease.
