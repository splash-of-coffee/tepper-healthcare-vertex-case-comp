# William Blair report on Vertex (March 26, 2026)

Source file: `materials\analyst-reports\AMKD Reports - MBA_[bull] William Blair 3.26.26.pdf`, 5 PDF pages. Page references are PDF pages, written p.N. Pages 1 and 2 hold the analysis. Pages 3 and 4 hold disclosures, and p.5 is the firm's research directory. All figures are as printed unless marked "calculated."

Terms used in this file:
- AMKD: APOL1-mediated kidney disease, kidney disease driven by two high-risk variants of the APOL1 gene.
- FSGS: focal segmental glomerulosclerosis, a scarring pattern in the kidney's filtering units.
- uPCR and uACR: urine protein-to-creatinine ratio and urine albumin-to-creatinine ratio, two urine measures of kidney damage. "Proteinuria" means protein in the urine.
- eGFR: estimated glomerular filtration rate, a blood-test estimate of kidney function. A surrogate endpoint is a lab measure the FDA accepts in place of a clinical outcome.
- IgAN: IgA nephropathy. RADAR: a study of kidney disease patient outcomes that the report cites.
- EPS: earnings per share. EV: enterprise value. P/E: share price divided by EPS.
- CF: cystic fibrosis. IP: intellectual property (patents).

## 2. AMKD details

### Launch year
- The report states no launch year for inaxaplin.

### Approval path and the endpoints the FDA wants
- William Blair writes that the FDA is "accepting proteinuria as a surrogate endpoint for approval in IgAN but requiring eGFR for AMKD currently" (p.2).
- The report expects "an interim 48-week eGFR-based readout ... potentially in late 2026" from the Phase 2/3 AMPLITUDE study (p.1).
- The report does not say whether it expects accelerated or full approval.
- The report writes: "Maze and other sponsors assume a ≥30% improvement on uACR is clinically meaningful, correlated to eGFR slope improvement" (p.2). It adds: "In the Phase IIa inaxaplin publication, Vertex sets that bar at ≥40% improvement in the discussion section" (p.2).
- The report cites an IgAN comparison. Novartis's atrasentan (Vanrafia) "narrowly missed on 2.5 year eGFR data despite showing 36% placebo-normalized proteinuria improvement at week 36" in the ALIGN study (p.2).

### Population definitions
- **AMPLITUDE population.** "Vertex is enrolling a broader APOL1 mutant, non-diabetic, proteinuric kidney disease population in the ongoing Phase II/III AMPLITUDE study" (p.1).
- **FSGS share.** The report calls FSGS "a smaller fragment of the potential AMKD market" (p.1). It gives no percentage.
- **Genetic risk by disease type.** The report writes that "the APOL1 genotype-mediated risk for FSGS onset (15- to 20-fold) appears greater than that for non-diabetic chronic kidney disease (7- to 10-fold...)" (p.1). The report cites Pollack MR et al., 2023, Glomerular Dis (p.1).
- **Diabetic versus non-diabetic.** The Maze data split patients into broad AMKD, patients without diabetes, and patients with FSGS (p.1). The figures appear below, under the Maze Therapeutics entry.
- The report gives no patient counts and no US or EU split. It does not mention the AMPLIFIED study.

## 3. Povetacicept and the kidney business
- The report gives no povetacicept details. It mentions the kidney business once: "With time, we should also see major contributions from Casgevy, Journavx, and the renal franchise" (p.2).
- The report does not discuss a sales force or other kidney programs.
- **Maze Therapeutics (MZE829).** The report describes the HORIZON Phase II data that Maze released "Before the market open on Wednesday, March 25" (p.1).
  - MZE829 is "a dual-mechanism APOL1 inhibitor" (p.1).
  - "Maze shares traded down 35% due to reduced proteinuria efficacy in non-FSGS patients" (p.1). The report lists Maze at $31.73 (p.1).
  - MZE829 at 250 mg for 12 weeks produced uPCR improvements of 31% in broad AMKD (n = 12), 46% in patients without diabetes (n = 7), and 59% in patients with FSGS (n = 4). These figures adjust for patient non-compliance (p.1).
  - The report adds that "Vertex's inaxaplin showed a 47.6% improvement in uPCR in FSGS patients (n=12) at week 13," citing Egbuna O et al., 2023, NEJM (p.1).
  - The report writes that "very small patient numbers ... make it difficult to conclude any superiority here" (p.1).

## 5. Risks, and the view on AMKD efficacy outside APOL1-FSGS
- **Named risks.** The report states that "Risks to our Outperform rating on Vertex are primarily clinical, regulatory, and commercial in nature" (p.2). It names risks tied to "the launch of exa-cel and potential commercial reimbursement pressures" (p.2). For Journavx, it sees "significant clinical risk associated with neuropathic indications" (p.2).
- **View on efficacy outside APOL1-FSGS.** William Blair expects a debate about the broader population.
  - The report writes that "the reduced efficacy in broader AMKD patients observed in the HORIZON study has raised investor concerns over the magnitude of efficacy" beyond FSGS (p.1).
  - The report says the difference in genetic risk "may imply differential efficacy of APOL1 inhibitors across populations," though "it remains difficult to interpret small patient numbers here" (p.1).
  - The report expects that "there will be debate over whether this will also be observed in AMPLITUDE" (p.1).
  - The report raises an "outstanding question overlooked by investors": how closely AMKD patients follow the kidney disease patterns "described in the RADAR study" (p.2).
  - The report asks whether a 31% proteinuria reduction in broader AMKD "can really translate into reliable eGFR benefit in a shorter 48-week timeframe" (p.2).
  - The report states: "We remain confident in inaxaplin's profile heading into the AMPLITUDE interim readout but acknowledge the broader patient population will fuel debate" (p.2).
