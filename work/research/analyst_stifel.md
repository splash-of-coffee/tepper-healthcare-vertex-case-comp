## 3. Povetacicept and the kidney business
- The report does not discuss povetacicept, a kidney sales force, or other kidney programs.
- The investment thesis on p.2 names "VX-147 (APOL1-FSGS)," "VX-880 (T1D cell replacement)," and "CTX001 (gene editing for sickle cell/beta thal)" as pipeline candidates. Stifel writes that "it's unclear to us whether" these candidates address markets "meaningful enough to be narrative changing" (p.2). The RBC (p.8) and Oppenheimer (p.19) reports label inaxaplin as VX-147.
- **Maze Therapeutics (MZE829).** Stifel describes the Maze Phase 2 data released on the day of the note (p.1).
  - MZE829 "is a once-daily oral small molecule that was evaluated in a ph2 basket study including AMKD patients with or without diabetes" (p.1).
  - "The study enrolled 14 AMKD patients." Twelve of them entered the efficacy analysis, made up of 4 non-diabetic FSGS, 3 non-diabetic non-FSGS, and 5 diabetic patients. The analysis excluded patients below the compliance threshold (p.1).
  - The full efficacy dataset showed a 36% mean reduction in uACR. The FSGS subgroup showed a 62% reduction, and the broader non-diabetic AMKD group showed a 49% reduction (p.1).
  - Maze argues that its FSGS efficacy exceeds inaxaplin's, citing "59% uPCR reduction @ 12 weeks vs 48% @ 13 weeks" (p.1).
  - Maze attributes the difference to its "dual mechanism," in which MZE829 "inhibits both APOL1 channel formation and function, while there's an argument that inaxaplin targets only the latter" (p.1). Stifel writes that "we still see this mechanistic thesis as a hypothesis" and that it is "cautious on these cross-trial comparisons given the small Ns" (p.1).
  - Only 1 of the 3 non-diabetic non-FSGS patients and 2 of the 5 diabetic patients reached a uACR improvement of at least 30%, which Stifel calls "clinically meaningful" (p.1).
- The Oppenheimer report (p.11) describes inaxaplin as inhibiting "APOL1 protein-mediated induction of channel formation." That wording differs from the argument Stifel reports here. This file records both statements as printed and does not judge between them.

## 5. Risks, and the view on AMKD efficacy outside APOL1-FSGS
- **Named risks.** "Risks to our target price include clinical risks, regulatory risks, commercial risks, manufacturing risks, and/or financing risks" (p.2). Stifel adds: "The success of Vertex's Triple combination is essential to our thesis" (p.2).
- **View on efficacy outside APOL1-FSGS.** Stifel treats efficacy outside FSGS as unresolved.
  - Stifel frames the question as "whether the very promising early FSGS results are extrapolatable to a more heterogeneous population (APOL1 with or without FSGS)" (p.1).
  - Stifel writes that "the FSGS subgroup is doing the heavy lifting" in the Maze data (p.1).
  - Stifel says the Maze results have "relevant implications for VRTX" because Vertex expanded AMPLITUDE beyond APOL1-FSGS (p.1).
  - Stifel asks whether patients in the other, more prevalent AMKD subtypes "have other non-APOL1 factors that are driving kidney injury that could put a ceiling on the efficacy" of APOL1 inhibition (p.1).
  - Stifel notes that "benefits may accrue with longer duration of treatment" and that "KOLs have also been very supportive of the APOL1 approach" (p.1).
  - Stifel concludes that "the pivotal study will need to derisk" non-FSGS efficacy and that "the answer remains TBD" (p.1).
