Notes:
1. The Stifel file name carries the date 5.25.26, but every page prints March 25, 2026 (p.1 to p.5). The file name is 61 days later than the printed date (calculated). The other five file-name dates match the printed dates.
2. RBC prints no inaxaplin POS. It says raising the POS to 100% would add 9% to its DCF fair value (p.5), and removing inaxaplin would subtract 6% (p.6). If inaxaplin's value scales in proportion to POS, these figures imply 6 ÷ (6 + 9) = 40% (calculated). This value rests on that assumption and on rounded inputs.
3. The unadjusted 2035 figures divide the printed risk-adjusted figure by the printed POS. For Morgan Stanley, $2.6bn ÷ 0.50 = $5.2bn. As a cross-check, 38,000 patients × $135,000 = $5.13bn (calculated). For Truist, $1,421.7M ÷ 0.70 = $2,031M (calculated).
4. Every discount rate in the table is a whole-company DCF rate. No report gives a rate specific to inaxaplin.
5. RBC carries the sponsor's "bear" tag and prints an Outperform rating (p.1). Stifel carries the "bear" tag and prints a Hold rating (p.1). Both "bull" reports print Outperform, and the "overview" reports print Overweight and Buy.
6. No report discusses primary care, referral, biopsy, or payer criteria for AMKD. Only Morgan Stanley mentions Vertex's free APOL1 genotyping and the AMKD ICD-10 codes effective 10/1/2025 (p.5).
7. Every report predates the July 6, 2026 Crinetics deal, the August 3, 2026 guidance raise, and the September 22, 2026 AMPLIFIED results. Five of the six also predate the June 1, 2026 povetacicept application acceptance. RBC (June 4) was printed after that date but does not mention the acceptance.
