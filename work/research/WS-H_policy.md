## Summary

- FDA kept its review deadlines through the 2025–2026 staff cuts and leadership changes. FDA's preliminary FY2025 data show 100% of reviews of original priority and standard new-molecular-entity applications finished on time (data to Sept 30, 2025), and FDA approved 44 novel drugs in 2026 through Sept 25. A mid-2027 accelerated-approval filing with priority review points to a decision about 8 months later, in early 2028; a national priority voucher would cut review to about 2–3 months (Casgevy was approved 53 days after filing on July 1, 2026).
- FDA's rule on investigational drugs (21 CFR 312.7) bars any promotion of inaxaplin before approval, and FDA cited it against Alar Pharmaceuticals on Aug 21, 2026. A hired or contracted sales force may still make unbranded "test and refer" calls and hand out sponsored-testing materials under the safeguards OIG accepted in Advisory Opinions 22-06 (Apr 6, 2022) and 24-12 (Dec 12, 2024). QOL Medical agreed on Nov 15, 2024 to pay $47M to resolve allegations that it sent free-test results to its sales force and tracked reps' conversion of positive tests.
- Unbranded APOL1 testing ads that name no drug fall outside FDA's prescription-drug ad rules. The rule FDA plans to propose in December 2026 (RIN 0910-AJ14) removes "adequate provision" only for branded broadcast ads. Vertex-paid EHR alerts have no Anti-Kickback Statute safe harbor; the EHR vendor Practice Fusion agreed on Jan 27, 2020 to pay $145M over an alert that an opioid maker paid about $1M to build.
- Medicare negotiation would first set inaxaplin's Medicare price in about 2037–2038, 9 years after a late-2027 or 2028 approval. No US orphan designation for inaxaplin was found. Vertex could still request one before its NDA, and the 2025 orphan exclusion (Public Law 119-21, section 71203, in force for 2028 prices) would then apply while every approved use stays inside the designation. The EPIC Act, which would add 4 years, is not law.
- Vertex is not among the 26 companies with most-favored-nation (MFN) pricing agreements (White House list of Aug 31, 2026). Signers give Medicaid MFN prices through the GENEROUS model, which reaches all 50 states from Sept 18, 2026, and commit to MFN prices on new medicines. The estimated effect on inaxaplin's blended US net price is 0–10%, most of it on Medicaid patients.

## 1. Medicare negotiation under the Inflation Reduction Act

### 1.1 Timing rules

The Inflation Reduction Act (IRA, 2022) lets Medicare set a negotiated price, called the "maximum fair price," for high-spending drugs that face no generic or biosimilar. A small-molecule drug becomes eligible once 7 years have passed since approval, measured on the date CMS (the Centers for Medicare & Medicaid Services) publishes its selection list. A biologic needs 11 years ([KFF](https://www.kff.org/medicare/faqs-about-the-inflation-reduction-acts-medicare-drug-price-negotiation-program/), updated Mar 11, 2026). CMS publishes each list on Feb 1, and the negotiated price starts on Jan 1 two years later. The gap from approval to a lower Medicare price is therefore about 9 years for small molecules and about 13 years for biologics. A small molecule needed approval by Feb 1, 2019 to be eligible for prices that start in 2028 (KFF).

CMS picks from the 50 Part B drugs and the 50 Part D drugs with the highest Medicare spending. It selected 15 drugs for 2028 and may select up to 20 a year from 2029 (KFF). The first 10 negotiated prices, effective 2026, were 38–79% below list price and 22% below 2023 net spending ([CMS](https://www.cms.gov/newsroom/fact-sheets/medicare-drug-price-negotiation-program-negotiated-prices-initial-price-applicability-year-2026), Aug 15, 2024). The next 15, effective 2027, cut net spending by 44%, or $12B ([CMS](https://www.cms.gov/newsroom/press-releases/cms-delivers-savings-seniors-15-major-drugs-cancer-chronic-disease), Nov 25, 2025).

Inaxaplin is a small molecule, so these rules set its timing (inference). An approval on or before Feb 1, 2028 makes it eligible for the Feb 1, 2035 list and a negotiated price from Jan 1, 2037. An approval from Feb 2, 2028 to Feb 1, 2029 moves the first negotiated price to Jan 1, 2038. Selection also requires inaxaplin to rank among the top Medicare spenders, and that rank depends on its Medicare share (handoff to WS-F). Povetacicept is a biologic, so its negotiated price would come about 13 years after approval (handoff to WS-G).

### 1.2 The EPIC Act and the small-molecule gap

The "Ensuring Pathways to Innovative Cures" (EPIC) Act would give small molecules the same 11-year eligibility as biologics. The House bill is H.R. 1492 and the Senate bill is S. 832, both introduced in early 2025 ([Congress.gov H.R. 1492](https://www.congress.gov/bill/119th-congress/house-bill/1492); [Congress.gov S. 832](https://www.congress.gov/bill/119th-congress/senate-bill/832)). Executive Order 14273 of Apr 15, 2025 directed HHS to work with Congress on the gap ([Epstein Becker Green](https://www.ebglaw.com/health-law-advisor/medicare-drug-price-negotiation-program-the-inflation-reduction-act-pill-penalty-and-other-ira-reforms-on-the-horizon-for-2026), May 2, 2025, secondary). Congress left the EPIC Act out of the July 4, 2025 budget law ([CRS R48633](https://www.congress.gov/crs-product/R48633)). Public Citizen still described the bill as pending on Jan 7, 2026 ([Public Citizen](https://www.citizen.org/article/patient-groups-big-pharma-medicare-price-negotiation-2026/)). This stream found no enactment through Sept 27, 2026, with medium confidence, because Congress.gov blocked automated reads. EPIC Act enactment would move inaxaplin's first negotiated price from about 2037–2038 to about 2041–2042 (inference).

### 1.3 The 2025 orphan-drug exclusion

Section 71203 of Public Law 119-21, the budget law signed July 4, 2025, widened the IRA's orphan-drug exclusion ([CRS R48633](https://www.congress.gov/crs-product/R48633); [Jones Day](https://www.jonesday.com/en/insights/2025/07/congress-expands-orphan-drug-exemptions-from-medicare-price-negotiations), July 2025, secondary). A drug is now excluded from negotiation if FDA has designated it for one or more rare diseases and every approved indication falls within those designations. The change applies to prices that start in 2028 or later. The 7-year eligibility period for a drug that later wins a non-orphan indication starts the day after that non-orphan approval, not at first approval. CBO estimated the section's cost to Medicare at $8.8B over 10 years ([CBO](https://www.cbo.gov/publication/61818), Oct 2025).

### 1.4 Inaxaplin's designations

The European Medicines Agency granted inaxaplin orphan designation and PRIME (priority medicines) status for AMKD (APOL1-mediated kidney disease). FDA granted Breakthrough Therapy designation and Rare Pediatric Disease designation for APOL1-mediated FSGS (focal segmental glomerulosclerosis, a scarring pattern in the kidney's filters) ([Vertex release](https://investors.vrtx.com/news-releases/news-release-details/vertex-announces-publication-new-england-journal-medicine), Mar 16, 2023; [Vertex FY2025 10-K](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/875320/000087532026000056/vrtx-20251231.htm)). This stream found no US orphan designation for inaxaplin. The check covered Vertex's FY2025 10-K, Vertex's releases, and web searches of FDA's orphan designation database. The database runs on a search form that automated tools cannot submit, so a team member should search it by hand for "inaxaplin" and "VX-147" (open question).

### 1.5 How the label and the designation interact

The rules below come from FDA's orphan-drug regulations. The bullets marked inference apply them to inaxaplin.

- A sponsor must request orphan designation before it submits the marketing application for that disease ([21 CFR 316.23(a)](https://www.law.cornell.edu/cfr/text/21/316.23)). Vertex's deadline is therefore its NDA (new drug application) submission, which the team projects for mid-2027.
- The disease must affect fewer than 200,000 people in the US. A subset of a larger disease qualifies only if properties of the drug make the other patients unsuitable candidates ([21 CFR 316.20(b)(6) and (b)(8)](https://www.law.cornell.edu/cfr/text/21/316.20)).
- Vertex states that AMKD affects about 150,000 people in the US and Europe combined ([Vertex](https://www.biospace.com/press-releases/vertex-announces-positive-results-from-phase-2b-amplified-study-of-inaxaplin-in-additional-populations-of-people-with-apol1-mediated-kidney-disease-amkd-and-completion-of-enrollment-in-the-phase-2-3-amplitude-trial), Sept 22, 2026). Maze states that AMKD affects more than 1 million people in the US alone ([Maze](https://www.globenewswire.com/news-release/2026/08/11/3343141/0/en/maze-therapeutics-reports-second-quarter-2026-financial-results-and-recent-highlights.html), Aug 11, 2026). FDA would judge prevalence from the sponsor's documentation and its own review.
- FDA's Rare Pediatric Disease designation requires a disease that is rare under the same 200,000 test ([21 U.S.C. 360ff(a)(3)](https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/21/360ff)). FDA therefore treated APOL1-mediated FSGS as rare (inference).
- FDA does not revoke a designation because prevalence later rises above 200,000 ([21 CFR 316.29(c)](https://www.law.cornell.edu/cfr/text/21/316.29)). More diagnoses from primary care testing would therefore not end a designation already granted (inference).
- The wording of the designation matters more than the breadth of the label (inference). A designation for "AMKD" would keep later AMKD label expansions, such as the modest-proteinuria group, inside the exclusion. A designation limited to APOL1-mediated FSGS would not cover a first label written as "AMKD with proteinuria," and the exclusion would not apply from launch.
- A label expansion outside the designation ends the exclusion and starts the 7-year period on that date. An orphan-only first label in 2028 followed by a non-orphan expansion in 2030 would move the first negotiated price to about 2039–2040 (inference).
- Orphan exclusivity blocks only the "same drug," which for a small molecule means the same active moiety ([21 CFR 316.3(b)(14)](https://www.law.cornell.edu/cfr/text/21/316.3)). Maze's MZE829 is a different molecule, so orphan exclusivity would not delay it (inference).
- Congress restored rare pediatric disease priority review vouchers on Feb 3, 2026, through Sept 30, 2029 ([Morgan Lewis](https://www.morganlewis.com/blogs/asprescribed/2026/03/fda-grants-first-voucher-under-restored-pediatric-disease-program), Mar 19, 2026, secondary). The statute denies a voucher when the original application seeks an adult indication ([21 U.S.C. 360ff(a)(4)](https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/21/360ff)). An inaxaplin NDA that includes adults would not earn a voucher (inference).

### 1.6 Direct effect on the options

The IRA lowers inaxaplin's Medicare price from about 2037–2038, 5 to 6 years after the 2027–2032 investment period ends. The cut reduces the value of each added patient only for treatment years after that date and only for the Medicare share. The cut scales with added patients in every combination, so it shrinks each combination's NPV (net present value) gain without changing their order (inference). A US orphan designation for AMKD, requested before the NDA, would delay the cut further. That request is a regulatory step Vertex can take whichever combination it funds (inference; Vertex regulatory review).

## 2. Most-favored-nation pricing, 2025–2026

Most-favored-nation (MFN) pricing ties a drug's US price to the lowest price paid in a set of peer countries. HHS set the target on May 20, 2025 as "the lowest price in an OECD country with a GDP per capita of at least 60 percent of the U.S. GDP per capita" ([Triage Health Law](https://www.triagehealthlawblog.com/pharmaceutical/trump-signs-executive-order-to-reduce-u-s-drug-prices-with-most-favored-nation-policy-and-cms-establishes-pricing-targets/), June 17, 2025, secondary, quoting HHS). The OECD is the Organisation for Economic Co-operation and Development.

### 2.2 Status of the Medicare models

GUARD (Guarding U.S. Medicare Against Rising Drug Costs) is a proposed mandatory Part D model ([Federal Register](https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/2025/12/23/2025-23705/guarding-us-medicare-against-rising-drug-costs-guard-model), Dec 23, 2025). It would run from Jan 1, 2027 through Dec 31, 2031. It covers sole-source drugs in 17 therapeutic categories whose Part D spending exceeds $69M in 2027 ([CMS](https://www.cms.gov/priorities/innovation/innovation-models/guard); [FDA Law Blog](https://www.thefdalawblog.com/2026/01/mfn-drug-pricing-update-after-generous-guard-and-globe-issue-from-cmss-innovation-center-part-i/), Jan 2026, secondary). CMS would apply it in randomly chosen ZIP code areas that hold about 25% of Part D enrollees. The manufacturer would pay a rebate equal to the amount its Medicare net price exceeds an international benchmark, minus any Part D inflation rebate. GLOBE (Global Benchmark for Efficient Drug Pricing) is the Part B version, proposed to start Oct 1, 2026 ([CMS](https://www.cms.gov/priorities/innovation/innovation-models/globe)).

Neither rule had gone to the Office of Management and Budget (OMB) by May 4, 2026, and GUARD needs a final rule by Nov 1, 2026 to start on time ([Applied Policy](https://www.appliedpolicy.com/cms-drug-pricing-models-encounter-delays-and-resistance/), May 2026, secondary). The CMS pages for both models still read "proposed" and were last changed Dec 29, 2025. This stream could not confirm a final rule for either model (open question).

Company releases on the Aug 31, 2026 deals say signers "will be exempt from future pricing mandates" ([Mintz](https://www.mintz.com/insights-center/viewpoints/2146/2026-09-23-pharmaceutical-policy-motion-continued-trump), Sept 23, 2026, secondary). A company that has not signed, such as Vertex, therefore stays exposed to GUARD and GLOBE if CMS finalizes them (inference). Mintz reports that the second-wave deals focus on Medicaid and that their terms are confidential.

### 2.3 Likely effect on a new launch's US net price

- The new-launch term asks signers to price new medicines at MFN levels. Companies have met it by raising ex-US prices or delaying ex-US launches rather than cutting US prices. Bristol Myers Squibb set Cobenfy's UK list price equal to its US list price on Sept 22, 2025 ([BMS](https://www.bms.com/news-and-media/statements/cobenfy-uk-pricing.html)). Reuters reported delayed European launches ([TipRanks summary of Reuters](https://www.tipranks.com/news/the-fly/drugmakers-delay-drug-launch-in-europe-over-u-s-pricing-policies-reuters-says-thefly-news), weak evidence). The likely effect on inaxaplin, if Vertex signs, is a later or higher-priced European launch with little change to the US list price (inference). Ex-US launch timing sits outside this stream (handoff to WS-G).
- The Medicaid term makes a signer or GENEROUS participant pay supplemental rebates so that Medicaid's net price does not exceed the MFN price ([CMS](https://www.cms.gov/priorities/innovation/innovation-models/generous); [White House](https://www.whitehouse.gov/fact-sheets/2026/09/fact-sheet-president-donald-j-trump-announces-lower-drug-prices-for-all-50-state-medicaid-programs/)). Patients found through primary care and safety-net clinics likely include more Medicaid enrollees than the brand average. The Medicaid term therefore lowers the value of patients added by the sales options and by added testing ads (Option 2) more than the value of baseline nephrology patients (inference; payer mix belongs to WS-F).
- GUARD, if finalized, would put an international-benchmark rebate on about 25% of inaxaplin's Part D volume. That result needs inaxaplin to fall in one of the 17 categories and to pass the spending threshold. Inaxaplin's category is not yet assigned (open question).
- The estimated blended cut to inaxaplin's US net price is 0–10%, with 3% as the base (estimate). The high case assumes Vertex signs, 20% of patients are on Medicaid with a 30-point added discount, and GUARD applies to 25% of a 30% Part D share at a 30% benchmark gap. The high-case arithmetic is 0.20 × 0.30 + 0.30 × 0.25 × 0.30 = 8.3%, rounded up to 10% for confidential launch terms. The base case assumes a Medicaid-only deal, a 15% Medicaid share, and a 20-point added discount, so 0.15 × 0.20 = 3%. The low case assumes Vertex does not sign and GUARD is never finalized.

### 2.4 Direct effect on the options

MFN policy lowers the value per added patient by an estimated 0–10%, with the largest cut on Medicaid-heavy patient groups (inference). The cut applies from launch, unlike the IRA cut, so it reaches the 2028–2035 revenue that pays back sales and advertising spending (inference).

## 3. FDA operations, review times, and approval rules

### 3.1 Staffing and leadership

- HHS cut about 3,500 FDA positions on Apr 1, 2025, about 19% of FDA staff. Review staff were exempt, but support staff were cut ([Skadden](https://www.skadden.com/insights/publications/2025/04/trump-administrations-first-100-days/mass-layoffs-at-fda), Apr 2025, secondary).
- FDA gained approval in June 2026 to hire 2,200 staff, and about 600 hires were secured by then ([BioSpace](https://www.biospace.com/fda/fda-hiring-2-200-people-to-staff-up-after-last-years-doge-cuts); [BioSpace](https://www.biospace.com/fda/despite-chaos-and-churn-fda-decisions-hold-mostly-steady-in-h1-2026), July 6, 2026).
- Commissioner Marty Makary resigned on May 12, 2026, and Kyle Diamantas, FDA's top food official, became acting commissioner ([STAT](https://www.statnews.com/2026/05/12/fda-commissioner-marty-makary-resigns-kyle-diamantas-acting/)).
- An acting director leads CDER (FDA's Center for Drug Evaluation and Research), the sixth person in that job since January 2025 ([BioSpace](https://www.biospace.com/fda/despite-chaos-and-churn-fda-decisions-hold-mostly-steady-in-h1-2026), July 6, 2026).

### 3.2 Review performance

- PDUFA (the Prescription Drug User Fee Act, under which companies pay review fees and FDA commits to deadlines) sets goals for new molecular entities (NMEs, drugs with an active ingredient FDA has not approved before). FDA acts on 90% of priority NME applications within 6 months of the 60-day filing date, about 8 months from submission. FDA acts on 90% of standard NME applications within 10 months of the filing date, about 12 months from submission. A major amendment during review can extend the goal date by 3 months ([PDUFA VII commitment letter](https://www.fda.gov/media/151712/download)).
- FDA's FY2025 PDUFA report shows FDA meeting or exceeding 8 of 10 review goals. It lists 100% of original priority and standard NME and BLA reviews on time, as preliminary data through Sept 30, 2025 ([FDA FY2025 PDUFA report](https://www.fda.gov/media/191112/download)). FDA met all 10 goals for FY2024. First-cycle approval rates for FY2024 filings were 76% for priority and 68% for standard applications.
- CDER approved 44 novel drugs in 2026 through Sept 25, compared with 46 in all of 2025 ([FDA](https://www.fda.gov/drugs/novel-drug-approvals-fda/novel-drug-approvals-2026); [RAPS](https://www.raps.org/resource/cder-approved-46-novel-drugs-in-2025-half-for-rar.html)). BioSpace counted 26 novel approvals in the first half of 2026 against 19 a year earlier, and 8 novel rejections against 6 ([BioSpace](https://www.biospace.com/fda/despite-chaos-and-churn-fda-decisions-hold-mostly-steady-in-h1-2026)).
- An aggregator site counted 28 of 29 sourced 2026 decisions on or before the goal date as of Sept 27, 2026 ([pdufa.bio](https://www.pdufa.bio/calendar), weak evidence; the count changed between two reads that day).
- FDA announced in February 2026 that one adequate and well-controlled trial plus confirmatory evidence is the default basis for approval ([BioPharma Dive](https://www.biopharmadive.com/news/fda-makary-prasad0one-pivotal-trial-nejm/812557/)). Makary and his deputy Vinay Prasad announced the policy in the New England Journal of Medicine. The policy supports a full approval that rests on AMPLITUDE alone if the accelerated path fails (inference).

### 3.3 A kidney precedent: sparsentan in FSGS

FDA extended Travere's Jan 13, 2026 goal date for sparsentan (Filspari) in FSGS by 3 months after a major amendment ([Travere](https://ir.travere.com/press-releases/news-details/2026/Travere-Therapeutics-Announces-FDA-Extends-Review-of-sNDA-for-FILSPARI-sparsentan-in-FSGS/default.aspx)). FDA then granted full approval on Apr 13, 2026 "to reduce proteinuria in adult and pediatric patients aged 8 years and older with focal segmental glomerulosclerosis (FSGS) without nephrotic syndrome" ([Travere](https://ir.travere.com/press-releases/news-details/2026/Travere-Therapeutics-Announces-Full-FDA-Approval-of-FILSPARI-sparsentan-the-First-and-Only-Approved-Medicine-for-FSGS/default.aspx)). Travere reported an eGFR (estimated glomerular filtration rate, a blood-test measure of kidney function) benefit of 1.1 mL/min/1.73 m² in the non-nephrotic patients. FDA therefore accepted proteinuria-based labeling in FSGS and limited the label to the subgroup with eGFR support (inference). The inaxaplin label could likewise come out narrower than the AMPLITUDE population, which would shrink the label-eligible count that every sales option targets (inference; handoff to WS-A).

### 3.4 Accelerated approval after FDORA

- FDORA (the Food and Drug Omnibus Reform Act, Dec 29, 2022) lets FDA require a confirmatory trial to be underway before accelerated approval and adds faster withdrawal procedures ([Cooley](https://www.cooley.com/news/insight/2023/2023-01-31-fdora-changes-to-the-fda-accelerated-approval-program), Jan 31, 2023, secondary).
- FDA's Jan 7, 2025 draft guidance says FDA "presumptively will, with only limited exceptions, require" the confirmatory trial to be underway before accelerated approval ([Arnold & Porter](https://www.arnoldporter.com/en/perspectives/advisories/2025/01/fda-guidance-confirmatory-trials-for-accelerated-approval), secondary; [Federal Register](https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/2025/01/07/2024-31527/accelerated-approval-and-considerations-for-determining-whether-a-confirmatory-trial-is-underway)). The guidance treats a trial as underway when enrollment has started, the target completion date fits timely conduct, and the sponsor's plans give assurance of completion. FDA may require full enrollment before approval when market access would slow enrollment.
- AMPLITUDE is a Phase 2/3 trial. Its 48-week interim analysis could support accelerated approval, and its final analysis uses eGFR slope after 2 years of treatment ([Vertex](https://www.biospace.com/press-releases/vertex-announces-positive-results-from-phase-2b-amplified-study-of-inaxaplin-in-additional-populations-of-people-with-apol1-mediated-kidney-disease-amkd-and-completion-of-enrollment-in-the-phase-2-3-amplitude-trial), Sept 22, 2026). Vertex completed full enrollment by that date.
- AMPLITUDE's continuing 2-year analysis meets the "underway" test, since enrollment is complete (inference). The 2-year result is the confirmatory evidence. The team timeline places it in early to mid 2028, within months of an accelerated approval (analyst expectation in the team timeline; not checked here).
- The accelerated-approval rule requires Vertex to submit all promotional materials for the first 120 days after approval during FDA's review. Vertex must submit each later piece at least 30 days before first use ([21 CFR 314.550](https://www.law.cornell.edu/cfr/text/21/314.550)).

### 3.5 Commissioner's National Priority Voucher (CNPV)

- FDA launched the CNPV pilot on June 17, 2025. The target review time is about 1–2 months after filing. The sponsor submits CMC data (chemistry, manufacturing, and controls) and draft labeling at least 60 days before the complete application, or 120 days ahead if foreign inspections are expected ([FDA FAQ](https://www.fda.gov/news-events/press-announcements/faqs-commissioners-national-priority-voucher-pilot-program), Feb 9, 2026).
- FDA awards vouchers under five priorities: public health crisis, "Innovative Breakthrough Therapies," large unmet medical need, onshoring, and affordability. Each voucher is tied to a specific application and cannot be transferred ([FDA program page](https://www.fda.gov/industry/commissioners-national-priority-voucher-cnpv-pilot-program), updated Aug 26, 2026).
- FDA named 9 recipients on Oct 16, 2025 and 6 more on Nov 6, 2025, including Casgevy ([HHS](https://www.hhs.gov/press-room/fda-awards-first-ever-national-priority-vouchers-nine-sponsors.html); [BioSpace](https://www.biospace.com/fda/wegovy-casgevy-among-latest-fda-priority-review-voucher-recipients)).
- FDA approved the Casgevy expansion on July 1, 2026, 53 days after filing, as the eighth approval under the program ([FDA](https://www.fda.gov/news-events/press-announcements/fda-approves-first-gene-therapy-young-children-sickle-cell-disease)).
- FDA held a public hearing on the program's criteria on June 4, 2026 ([Federal Register](https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/2026/03/23/2026-05573/commissioners-national-priority-voucher-cnpv-pilot-program-public-hearing-request-for-comments), Mar 23, 2026).
- Vertex could apply for inaxaplin under the unmet-need or breakthrough priorities (inference). The program is a pilot run from the commissioner's office. Its founding commissioner left on May 12, 2026, and the Casgevy approval on July 1 shows the program kept running after his exit. A law-firm summary of the PDUFA VIII proposal does not mention the program ([Arnold & Porter](https://www.arnoldporter.com/en/perspectives/advisories/2026/08/fda-releases-proposed-pdufa-viii-commitment-letter), Aug 19, 2026, secondary), so the program has no user-fee commitment behind it (inference; medium).

### 3.6 PDUFA VIII

PDUFA VII expires Sept 30, 2027. FDA published its PDUFA VIII proposal on Aug 14, 2026, held a public meeting on Sept 16, 2026, and takes comments until Oct 16, 2026 ([Federal Register](https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/2026/08/14/2026-16650/reauthorization-of-the-prescription-drug-user-fee-act-public-meeting-request-for-comments)). FDA sends its recommendations to Congress by Jan 15, 2027 ([Holland & Knight](https://www.hklaw.com/en/insights/publications/2026/08/fda-releases-draft-gdufa-pdufa-commitment-letters-as-user-fee), secondary). PDUFA VII itself became law on Sept 30, 2022, the day the prior authorization expired ([FDA FY2025 PDUFA report](https://www.fda.gov/media/191112/download)). An inaxaplin NDA filed in mid-2027 would be under review on the expiry date, so a lapse would put fee-funded review staff at risk (inference; low probability).

### 3.8 Direct effect on the options

FDA is meeting its deadlines, so FDA operations add little risk to the launch date (base case: 0 added months). The 120-day pre-submission rule means launch materials for any sales option and any branded advertising must be finished and sent to FDA before the decision date (inference from 21 CFR 314.550).

## 4. Direct-to-consumer advertising

### 4.1 September 2025 enforcement

- The President signed the "Memorandum on Addressing Misleading Direct-to-Consumer Prescription Drug Advertisements" on Sept 9, 2025 ([GovInfo DCPD-202500906](https://www.govinfo.gov/app/details/DCPD-202500906)).
- FDA said it was sending "thousands of letters" to drug sponsors and "approximately 100 cease-and-desist letters" ([Covington](https://www.cov.com/en/news-and-insights/insights/2025/10/fda-advertising-and-promotion-enforcement-activities-update), Oct 17, 2025, secondary, quoting FDA). CDER posted more than 50 warning letters and more than 50 untitled letters in that quarter. The letters mainly targeted TV ads, and others addressed physician webpages, print ads, websites, and marketers of compounded drugs.
- FDA's first-quarter 2026 actions were 9 untitled letters and 1 warning letter to drug companies, more than 30 warning letters to compounders, and 4 untitled letters from CBER (FDA's biologics center). TV ads remained the main target ([Covington](https://www.cov.com/en/news-and-insights/insights/2026/04/fda-advertising-and-promotion-enforcement-activities-update), Apr 20, 2026, secondary).

### 4.2 The planned broadcast rule

- The federal regulatory agenda lists "Transparency in Direct-to-Consumer Advertising" (RIN 0910-AJ14) as a proposed rule expected in December 2026 ([reginfo.gov](https://www.reginfo.gov/public/do/eAgendaViewRule?pubId=202510&RIN=0910-AJ14)). The rule would revise 21 CFR 202.1 to end "adequate provision," the option that lets TV and radio ads send viewers to a website, phone number, or print ad instead of carrying the full brief summary of risks. FDA classes it as economically significant, OMB's label for rules with effects of $100M or more a year.
- Law firms expect the rule to end most branded TV ads. Latham wrote that it "could function as a de facto ban on DTC broadcast advertising" ([Latham](https://www.lw.com/en/insights/fda-proposes-to-eliminate-adequate-provision-standard-for-dtc-broadcast-advertising), July 14, 2026, secondary). The rule covers broadcast media, and its reach to streaming, digital, and social ads is unclear ([Foley Hoag](https://foleyhoag.com/news-and-insights/publications/alerts-and-updates/2026/july/fda-proposes-to-eliminate-adequate-provision-for-broadcast-prescription-drug-advertising/), July 7, 2026; [FDA Law Blog](https://www.thefdalawblog.com/2026/07/coming-soon-proposed-rule-to-remove-adequate-provision-and-ban-dtc-tv-ads/), July 7, 2026; both secondary).
- FDA has not yet published the proposal. A final rule needs a comment period and OMB review, and industry litigation is likely, so the rule may not be in force at an early-2028 launch (inference; see Candidate assumptions).

### 4.3 Unbranded communications

- FDA defines disease awareness communications as those that "discuss a particular disease or health condition, but do not mention any specific drug or device or make any representation or suggestion concerning a particular drug or device." FDA calls the consumer version "help-seeking" communications ([FDA CBER Q&A](https://www.fda.gov/vaccines-blood-biologics/labeling-cber-regulated-products/advertising-promotional-labeling-questions-and-answers)).
- FDA withdrew its 2004 draft guidance on help-seeking and disease awareness communications on May 6, 2015, with other old drafts, and has not replaced it ([Federal Register](https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/2015/05/06/2015-10477/withdrawal-of-draft-guidance-documents-published-before-december-31-2013)). The 2004 draft had said FDA could regulate unbranded ads that look like or link to branded ads, and unbranded ads from the maker of the only drug in its class ([PLoS Medicine](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC1434509/), 2006).
- FDA's drug-ad rule (21 CFR 202.1) applies to ads for a drug, so an ad that names no drug and suggests none falls outside it, including the planned broadcast change (inference; medium-high).
- The FTC's ban on deceptive advertising still applies to unbranded ads (inference; medium).
- A 2026 Maryland bill (HB 1133) would require manufacturers to register disease awareness campaigns and name the related drug. The House Health Committee heard it on Mar 12, 2026, and it has not passed ([Maryland General Assembly](https://mgaleg.maryland.gov/mgawebsite/Legislation/Details/hb1133)).

### 4.4 What an unbranded APOL1 testing campaign may and may not say

The table reflects FDA's written rules and the OIG opinions on sponsored testing described in section 6. It is not legal advice, and every piece needs medical-legal-regulatory (MLR) review.

| Period | May say | May not say |

The planned broadcast rule does not reach added testing ads (Option 2) if they stay unbranded (inference). Vertex will make the only APOL1 inhibitor after approval, so its unbranded ads need stricter separation from branded materials than a company with competitors would need (inference; legal review).

## 5. Promotion before approval

### 5.1 The rules

- FDA's rule states that a sponsor "shall not represent in a promotional context that an investigational new drug is safe or effective for the purposes for which it is under investigation or otherwise promote the drug." The same rule allows "full exchange of scientific information concerning the drug, including dissemination of scientific findings in scientific or lay media" ([21 CFR 312.7(a)](https://www.law.cornell.edu/cfr/text/21/312.7)).
- FDA enforces the rule at medical meetings. OPDP (FDA's Office of Prescription Drug Promotion) sent Alar Pharmaceuticals an untitled letter dated Aug 21, 2026 over an exhibit booth and brochure at the American Psychiatric Association meeting of May 16–20, 2026 ([FDA letter](https://www.fda.gov/media/194514/download); [FDA Law Blog](https://www.thefdalawblog.com/2026/09/opdps-latest-reminder-on-preapproval-promotion/), Sept 2, 2026).
- Payers use a separate channel. FD&C Act section 502(gg), added by the Consolidated Appropriations Act, 2023, lets companies give payers and formulary committees information about investigational products with required disclosures ([Skadden](https://www.skadden.com/insights/publications/2023/01/trend-toward-broader-communication-continues), Jan 2023, secondary). FDA issued an updated draft guidance on payer communications in June 2026, with comments due Aug 3, 2026 ([National Law Review](https://natlawreview.com/article/fda-updates-8-year-old-payor-communications-guidance), June 11, 2026, secondary). The payer channel does not cover primary care physicians (PCPs).

The table applies to a hired primary care force (Option 1a), a contracted force (Option 1b), and nephrology reps who add top PCP targets (Option 1c). The rep column is inference from 21 CFR 312.7 and the OIG opinions on sponsored testing. The medical science liaison (MSL) column reflects standard industry practice (inference). All of it needs legal review.

|---|---|---|

| "Test and refer" message to a PCP | Yes, if it names no drug and rests on current uses of testing | Yes |

| Discuss inaxaplin data unprompted | No | No promotion; scientific exchange at scientific meetings and with investigators |

### 5.3 Direct answer on "test and refer" calls

Unbranded "test and refer" disease-education calls to PCPs are allowed before approval (inference; legal review). Each call must name no drug and imply no coming treatment. The materials must pass MLR review. The OIG sponsored-testing safeguards in section 6 must apply to any kits or program materials. Rep pay and goals must rest on activity, not on tests ordered or positive results. Reps must route every drug question to Medical Affairs. The nephrology reps in Option 1c would also detail branded povetacicept if FDA approves it on Nov 30, 2026, so their calls must keep unbranded APOL1 content apart from branded content (inference).

### 5.4 Direct effect on the options

A primary care force of any type can do testing and referral work before approval, and nothing more (inference). The return on a pre-approval force therefore comes from added tests, not from early prescriptions, which favors a small pilot before approval that scales after approval (inference).

## 6. Legal limits on manufacturer-sponsored programs

### 6.1 The laws

- The Anti-Kickback Statute (AKS, 42 U.S.C. 1320a-7b(b)) makes it a crime to offer or pay anything of value to induce the ordering or recommending of items that federal health programs pay for. OIG (the HHS Office of Inspector General) treats a free test as remuneration under the AKS in each sponsored-testing opinion ([AO 22-06](https://oig.hhs.gov/documents/advisory-opinions/1028/AO-22-06.pdf); [AO 24-12](https://oig.hhs.gov/documents/advisory-opinions/10117/AO-24-12.pdf)).
- The beneficiary inducement CMP (civil monetary penalty) covers offers to Medicare or Medicaid patients that are likely to influence their choice of provider. OIG applied it to a manufacturer's free genetic test because the test could steer the patient's follow-up care to the ordering physician (AO 22-06).
- OIG advisory opinions (AOs) bind only the requester. They show which facts OIG treats as low risk.

### 6.2 OIG opinions on sponsored testing

| Opinion | Issued | Arrangement | Facts OIG relied on |
|---|---|---|---|

| AO 24-12 | Dec 12, 2024 | Free genetic testing, counseling, and disease education for an ultra-rare genetic kidney condition | An unbranded public website describes the condition and the free testing and names the sponsor; OIG found that "purely educational" awareness content is not remuneration; the manufacturer receives no identifiable data; counselors may not discuss therapy; OIG warned it "likely would reach a different conclusion" if shared data allowed targeted marketing |
| AO 25-07 | July 2, 2025 | Free companion lab test before prescribing | Reps do not distribute materials based on test use; the lab may not promote the program; the manufacturer does not proactively tell providers or patients about it ([Epstein Becker Green](https://www.ebglaw.com/health-law-advisor/oig-advisory-opinion-25-07-offers-a-favorable-path-for-patient-access-through-sponsored-testing), secondary) |

QOL Medical and its CEO paid $47M on Nov 15, 2024 to resolve kickback allegations over free breath tests ([DOJ release via FDA](https://www.fda.gov/inspections-compliance-enforcement-and-criminal-investigations/doj-press-releases-involving-fda-oci/qol-medical-and-its-ceo-agree-pay-47-million-allegedly-paying-kickbacks-induce-claims-qols-drug)). Vertex's Labcorp APOL1 program already follows the OIG pattern. The program shares no identifiable patient or provider data with Vertex, bills no patient or payer, and requires no prescribing ([Labcorp program page](https://labcorpapol1test.com/), read Sept 27, 2026).

### 6.3 Direct effect on the options

- The sales options (Options 1a, 1b, and 1c) can carry testing information and kits under the limits in AO 22-06, but reps cannot see test-order data or earn pay tied to tests (inference).
- Added testing ads (Option 2) run into the fact that OIG's favorable opinions rested on limited patient outreach. AO 22-06 and AO 25-07 had none, and AO 24-12 allowed one unbranded website. Paid media that advertises "free APOL1 testing from Vertex" to patients goes beyond those facts (inference). A campaign that tells patients to ask a clinician about APOL1 testing, without a free-test offer, carries less AKS risk (inference). Vertex counsel should review the creative, and Vertex could request its own advisory opinion.
- Family (cascade) testing has a precedent. AO 22-06 made relatives of confirmed patients eligible for the free test with a physician's attestation. Consent rules belong to WS-E.

### 6.4 EHR and lab-report prompts

- Practice Fusion, an EHR vendor, agreed on Jan 27, 2020 to pay $145M, including a $25.4M criminal fine, about $1M in forfeiture, and $118.6M in civil settlements ([DOJ](https://www.justice.gov/archives/opa/pr/electronic-health-records-vendor-pay-145-million-resolve-criminal-and-civil-investigations-0); [National Law Review](https://natlawreview.com/article/doj-announces-settlement-ehr-company-to-resolve-criminal-and-civil-kickback), secondary). An opioid maker paid Practice Fusion nearly $1M to build a clinical decision support (CDS) alert that pushed extended-release opioids. The alert fired about 230 million times from July 2016 to spring 2019 ([DOJ statement of facts](https://www.justice.gov/d9/press-releases/attachments/2020/01/27/2-4-_exhibit_c_to_deferred_prosecution_agreement-_statement_of_facts.pdf); [Fierce Healthcare](https://www.fiercehealthcare.com/tech/allscripts-practice-fusion-to-pay-145m-settlement-doj-opioid-case), secondary).
- Purdue Pharma, the opioid maker, pleaded guilty in 2020 to conspiracies to violate the AKS that included the Practice Fusion payments ([DOJ](https://www.justice.gov/archives/opa/pr/opioid-manufacturer-purdue-pharma-pleads-guilty-fraud-and-kickback-conspiracies)).
- OIG's 2020 value-based safe harbors exclude pharmaceutical manufacturers ([Crowell & Moring](https://www.crowell.com/en/insights/client-alerts/oig-issues-final-rule-to-update-aks-safe-harbors-part-ii), secondary; [Federal Register](https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/2020/12/02/2020-26072/medicare-and-state-health-care-programs-fraud-and-abuse-revisions-to-safe-harbors-under-the), Dec 2, 2020). The EHR donation safe harbor does not protect drug makers as donors ([Jones Day](https://www.jonesday.com/en/insights/2021/01/oig-and-cms-modify-aks-safe-harbor-and-stark-law-exception-for-ehr-donations), Jan 2021, secondary).
- A Vertex-paid EHR alert or lab-report comment that prompts APOL1 testing therefore has no safe harbor, and its risk depends on intent, design control, and payment terms (inference; legal review). A paid lab-report prompt also conflicts with the favorable opinions, which barred the lab from promoting the sponsored program (inference). The lower-risk design is a protocol that a health system builds and owns, based on independent guidelines, with no Vertex payment or content control (inference; legal review).

### 6.5 Sales-force pay: employees versus contractors

- OIG's 2003 compliance guidance for drug makers says a pay arrangement with a sales agent "potentially implicates the anti-kickback statute" and points to the employee and personal-services safe harbors ([Federal Register](https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/2003/05/05/03-10949/oig-compliance-program-guidance-for-pharmaceutical-manufacturers), May 5, 2003).
- The employee safe harbor protects pay to bona fide employees. The personal-services safe harbor requires the pay method to be set in advance, at fair market value, and not based on the volume or value of business generated ([Jones Day](https://www.jonesday.com/en/insights/2021/02/recent-changes-to-the-antikickback-statutes-personal-services-safe-harbor), Feb 2021, secondary).
- The Fourth Circuit held in United States v. Mallory (2021) that commissions paid to independent-contractor marketers fell outside the employee safe harbor ([ArentFox Schiff](https://www.afslaw.com/perspectives/health-care-counsel-blog/aks-hot-zone-contract-sales-forces-and-the-limits-incentive), Jan 6, 2026, secondary).
- Vertex should pay a contract sales organization (CSO) running Option 1b fixed, fair-market-value fees for activity, such as calls, reach, and training, and should avoid fees tied to sales, prescriptions, or tests (inference; legal review). The hired force (Option 1a) and the nephrology reps (Option 1c) are Vertex employees, so their commission plans can fit the employee safe harbor.

### 6.6 HIPAA and targeting

- HIPAA covers health plans, providers such as labs and physicians, and their business associates. Vertex is not a covered entity for these programs (inference).
- A covered entity needs the patient's written authorization to use or disclose protected health information (PHI) for marketing, and the authorization must state any payment from a third party. A sale of PHI also needs authorization ([45 CFR 164.508(a)(3)–(4)](https://www.law.cornell.edu/cfr/text/45/164.508)).
- De-identified data fall outside HIPAA ([45 CFR 164.514](https://www.law.cornell.edu/cfr/text/45/164.514)). AO 22-06 relied on program data that met the de-identification standard and on a ban on using even those data for sales targeting.
- Vertex may target physicians with de-identified or aggregate data, such as claims counts by practice (inference). Vertex may not use sponsored-test data, identified or not, to target physicians or patients under the OIG pattern (AO 22-06; AO 24-12). Patient-level ad targeting through data sources outside HIPAA falls under FTC and state privacy law, which WS-E covers.

## Effect on the recommendation

The policy findings support the working hypothesis, a contracted primary care force (Option 1b) plus added unbranded testing ads (Option 2) scaled in stages, and they add design limits. FDA rules favor staging. A mid-2027 filing with priority review points to a decision about 8 months later, and the 2-year AMPLITUDE result, which FDA can use under FDORA to confirm or withdraw an accelerated approval, is expected within months of launch (team timeline). A fixed build made before approval loses its value if FDA withdraws the approval, while staged spending limits that loss to what Vertex spent before the readout. FDA's investigational-drug rule (21 CFR 312.7) limits any rep, hired or contracted, to unbranded testing and referral work before approval, so a pre-approval force earns its return only through added tests. The contracted force (Option 1b) fits a staged plan, and the one legal cost specific to it is the pay structure. Vertex should pay the CSO fixed, fair-market-value fees for activity, because volume-based fees to contractors fall outside the Anti-Kickback Statute safe harbors (United States v. Mallory, 2021). The hired force (Option 1a) gains one legal advantage, commission pay protected by the employee safe harbor, but it pays full-time reps who cannot mention the drug until approval, which weakens it before 2028. Retargeting the nephrology reps (Option 1c) faces no legal barrier, but those reps would also detail branded povetacicept from December 2026 if FDA approves it, and they must keep APOL1 content unbranded and separate (inference). The added testing ads (Option 2) fall outside FDA's drug-ad rules and outside the planned December 2026 broadcast rule when they name no drug and imply no coming treatment, and a restriction on branded TV would raise their share of post-approval promotion (inference). Option 2's main legal limit is the Anti-Kickback Statute. Paid media that offers Vertex's free test to patients goes beyond the facts in OIG's favorable opinions (AO 22-06 had no patient outreach; AO 24-12 allowed one website), so the ads should tell patients to ask about testing, and counsel should clear any free-test offer. Vertex-paid EHR or lab-report prompts have no safe harbor and carry the Practice Fusion precedent ($145M in 2020, plus Purdue's guilty plea), so any EHR element should be a protocol the health system builds and owns (inference; legal review). Pricing policy trims the value of each added patient by an estimated 0–10% from launch (MFN Medicaid terms, and GUARD if finalized) and starts Medicare negotiation around 2037–2038. Both effects scale with added patients in every combination, so neither changes the ranking of the eight combinations, although patients found in Medicaid-heavy primary care settings lose more value than the brand average (inference). The findings, taken together, support Option 1b plus Option 2 with staged scale-up, weaken Option 1a, and leave Option 1c legal but limited by the povetacicept launch. They also add three design rules. Reps and contractors earn activity-based pay. No sponsored-test data flows to sales. Unbranded ads stay visually separate from branded launch materials.

### Statistics flagged

- A search-engine summary stated that CDER approved 7 novel drugs through August 2026. FDA's own page lists 44 through Sept 25, 2026. The 7 figure is wrong and is not used.
- White House fact sheets say signers cover 90% of branded sales and that MFN deals save "more than $600 billion." AHA reports about 89%, which this file uses. The fact sheets give no method for the $600B figure, so it is not used.
- Vendor blogs (IntuitionLabs, PRV Watch) give national-priority-voucher review times of 44 and 55 days for other products. FDA pages confirm only Casgevy's 53 days, so the vendor figures are not used.
- The pdufa.bio tally changed between two reads on Sept 27, 2026, from 32 decisions with 3 late to 29 with 1 late. It is weak evidence.
- FDA's FY2025 review figures are preliminary; final FY2025 figures will appear in the FY2026 report.

### Handoffs

- **WS-A (patient numbers):** US AMKD prevalence under FDA's 200,000 test, with Vertex's 150,000 (US and Europe) against Maze's 1 million or more (US); the chance that the first label is narrower than the AMPLITUDE population, as happened with sparsentan in FSGS.
- **WS-B (Vertex capabilities):** whether Vertex holds or will request a US orphan designation before the NDA; plans for a national priority voucher; MSL team size and the medical-information process; Vertex's MFN stance; the compliance design of the Labcorp, Natera, and Arkana programs.
- **WS-C (sales-force costs):** CSO fees must be fixed, fair-market-value activity fees; pre-approval rep goals must be activity-based; launch materials must be finished and submitted during FDA review (21 CFR 314.550); MLR review time and cost.
- **WS-D (advertising channels and costs):** branded TV may be unavailable at launch (30% base chance the broadcast rule is in force); unbranded testing ads are unaffected; whether paid media may mention the free test (legal review); Maryland HB 1133 disclosure; visual separation from branded creative.
- **WS-E (equity, privacy, consent):** consent design for cascade testing (AO 22-06 covers relatives); FTC and state privacy law for patient-level ad targeting outside HIPAA.
- **WS-F (payer coverage and gross-to-net):** payer mix by channel, needed to apply the IRA cut from 2037–2038 and the MFN Medicaid cut; GENEROUS participation; GUARD scope; payer communications before approval under 502(gg).
- **WS-G (Vertex portfolio):** povetacicept is a biologic, so Medicare negotiation comes about 13 years after approval, and FDA decides on it Nov 30, 2026; Casgevy already used a national priority voucher; inaxaplin's patent expiry, which the FY2025 10-K does not state; ex-US launch timing under MFN terms.
- **WS-I (primary care launch examples):** AO 22-06 matches Pfizer's tafamidis (ATTR-CM) sponsored-testing program, and AO 24-12 matches a primary hyperoxaluria type 1 drug approved for ages 9 and up (both inferences, since OIG redacts names); QOL Medical as a negative example; testing volumes before and after approval in those programs.
