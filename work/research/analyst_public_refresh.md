# Vertex (VRTX) public analyst and management refresh, June to September 2026

Prepared Sept 27, 2026 for the Tepper Healthcare Case Competition team. This file records public analyst actions, consensus figures and Vertex statements dated after the seven sponsor reports. The sponsor PDFs were not opened or quoted.

Terms used in this file:
- AMKD is APOL1-mediated kidney disease, kidney disease caused by two variants of the APOL1 gene.
- PT is a 12-month share price target. POS is probability of success, an analyst's estimate that a drug reaches approval in a given population.
- UACR and UPCR are the urine albumin-to-creatinine and urine protein-to-creatinine ratios. Both measure protein leaking into urine.
- eGFR is estimated glomerular filtration rate, a measure of kidney function. IA is interim analysis.
- T2D is type 2 diabetes. CF is cystic fibrosis. IgAN is IgA nephropathy. CI is confidence interval.
- A PDUFA date is the FDA's target date to act on an application. A BLA is a Biologics License Application.

Confidence scale:
- High means an original source (Vertex release, SEC filing, call transcript) or two independent reputable outlets agree.
- Medium means one reputable news outlet, or an aggregator page labeled "secondary" (TipRanks, The Fly, MarketBeat, MarketScreener, Yahoo Finance, stockanalysis.com).
- Low means one secondary or machine-written source, or sources that conflict.

Investing.com rating and conference articles state they were "generated with the support of AI and reviewed by an editor." This file marks them "AI-assisted." A MarketBeat conference summary states it was "generated by narrative science technology." This file marks it "machine-written."

## 1. Summary

- Six of the seven sponsor firms raised their PTs after their report dates, and none changed its rating. Jefferies went from $580 to $640 (Buy), Morgan Stanley from $612 to $665 (Overweight), Oppenheimer from $540 to $600 (Outperform), RBC from $543 to $570 (Outperform), Truist from $542 to $565 (Buy) and Stifel from $466 to $500 (Hold). William Blair reiterated Outperform and publishes no PT.
- The 45% POS for the diabetic population comes from a Guggenheim Securities note dated Aug 3, 2026, which put POS for primary AMKD at 65% (Fierce Biotech, Sept 22). No public source attributes a 45% figure to BMO. BMO's Sept 22 note, as quoted by Investing.com and BioSpace, contains no POS number.
- Jefferies raised its inaxaplin AMKD peak-sales estimate from about $3.1 billion to about $3.6 billion (16% higher) on Sept 23. Leerink estimates inaxaplin sales of $1.2 billion in 2035. No public consensus for inaxaplin sales was found.
- AMPLIFIED cut UACR by 42.7% at week 13 in AMKD with modest proteinuria (23 patients) and by 17.3% in AMKD with T2D (18 patients). Vertex still guides the AMPLITUDE IA to early 2027, as it did on May 4. Vertex investor relations mentioned a possible second renal launch around September 2027 (AI-assisted summary, to confirm).
- Vertex's Sept 22 release puts AMKD at about 150,000 people in the U.S. and Europe combined. Vertex investor relations said the two AMPLIFIED populations could add about 100,000. An AI-assisted summary quotes the CEO on Sept 14 as saying 100,000 to 150,000 in the United States alone, which conflicts with the release.

## 6. What changed since the sponsor reports

**Probability of success.** Guggenheim Securities put POS at 65% for primary AMKD and 45% for AMKD with T2D in an Aug 3, 2026 note, according to Fierce Biotech on Sept 22 (https://www.fiercebiotech.com/biotech/vertexs-midstage-data-amplify-kidney-disease-market-opportunity). Fierce's exact wording is "The analysts see the Type 2 diabetes population as a longer shot, ascribing a 45% chance of success to the indication." Two search approaches found no BMO POS figure. BMO's Sept 22 note calls the diabetes result "directionally encouraging" and gives no percentage. The Guggenheim note predates the Sept 22 AMPLIFIED data. H.C. Wainwright had expected at least a 22% UACR cut in the diabetes cohort, and the 17.3% result fell 4.7 percentage points short, with a CI that crossed zero. H.C. Wainwright had expected 30–40% in the modest-proteinuria cohort, and the 42.7% result exceeded the top of that range by 2.7 points. UBS wrote that the modest-proteinuria result "de-risk[s]" AMPLITUDE (BioPharma Dive, Sept 24). The CEO said on Aug 3 that Vertex has an FDA agreement for potential accelerated approval based on one-year eGFR at the IA. No firm published a revised POS after Sept 22 in the sources found.

**Launch timing.** Vertex guided the AMPLITUDE IA to "early 2027" in its May 4 Q1 release, its Aug 3 Q2 release and its Sept 22 release (https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/875320/000087532026000171/ex-991_q12026.htm and the Section 5 links). The Sept 22 release reports AMPLITUDE fully enrolled, which the Aug 3 release had guided for the second half of 2026. The CEO said on Aug 3 that a positive IA would let Vertex "file for potential accelerated approval in the U.S. thereafter." Jefferies wrote on Sept 23 that the IA is expected in the first half of 2027. An AI-assisted summary of the Sept 9 Wells Fargo conference quotes investor relations as saying "Potentially September 2027, getting close to a second launch potentially." The team should confirm that wording from the webcast replay before using September 2027 as a launch month. Povetacicept in IgAN, the first renal launch, has a PDUFA date of Nov 30, 2026.

**Label scope.** AMPLITUDE enrolls people with primary AMKD. Vertex's Q1 release calls the two AMPLIFIED groups "populations not being studied in the AMPLITUDE trial," and Stifel notes that the pivotal trial excludes diabetic patients. Vertex's Sept 22 release puts AMKD at about 150,000 people in the U.S. and Europe combined. Fierce Biotech reports Vertex's head of investor relations as saying a label covering both AMPLIFIED arms would add about 100,000 patients. An AI-assisted summary quotes the CEO on Sept 14 as saying 100,000 to 150,000 people in the United States, which conflicts with the release's U.S.-plus-Europe basis. UBS cites studies that place about 80% of AMKD patients in the modest-proteinuria range. The diabetes cohort's UACR cut (17.3%) was 25.4 percentage points below the modest-proteinuria cohort's cut (42.7%). Inference: a first label based on AMPLITUDE would cover primary AMKD without diabetes, and the modest-proteinuria group has the stronger data for a later expansion.

**Peak or 2035 sales.** Jefferies raised its inaxaplin AMKD peak-sales estimate from about $3.1 billion to about $3.6 billion on Sept 23. The increase is $0.5 billion, or 16% (Investing.com, AI-assisted; confirm in the Jefferies note). Leerink estimated inaxaplin sales of $1.2 billion in 2035 (BioSpace, Sept 22). The Jefferies peak figure is 3.0 times the Leerink 2035 figure. The two figures measure different things (peak year versus calendar 2035), and neither public source says whether its figure is risk-adjusted. Oppenheimer estimated on May 5 that povetacicept and inaxaplin together would bring about $9.4 billion of revenue by 2035, against about $11 billion from CF, with no split by drug. No public consensus for inaxaplin sales was found.
