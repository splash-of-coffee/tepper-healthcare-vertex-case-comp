# Truist Securities report on Vertex (May 26, 2026)

Source file: `materials\analyst-reports\AMKD Reports - MBA_[overview] Truist 5.26.26.pdf`, 11 PDF pages. Page references are PDF pages, written p.N. Pages 4 to 8 hold the model exhibits (guidance, estimates, income statement, balance sheet, cash flow, DCF). Pages 9 to 11 hold the thesis, valuation, and disclosures. All figures are as printed unless marked "calculated."

Terms used in this file:
- AMKD: APOL1-mediated kidney disease, kidney disease driven by two high-risk variants of the APOL1 gene.
- FSGS: focal segmental glomerulosclerosis, a scarring pattern in the kidney's filtering units.
- PoS: probability of success, the chance the analyst assigns that the drug reaches the market. PoS-adjusted ("PoS-Adj") sales are forecast sales multiplied by the PoS.
- TAM: total addressable market. MoA: mechanism of action. PoC: proof of concept.
- DCF: discounted cash flow valuation. EV: enterprise value.
- BLA: biologics license application. PRV: priority review voucher, which shortens FDA review. PDUFA date: the FDA's target date for an approval decision.
- IgAN: IgA nephropathy, the first indication for povetacicept ("pove"). pMN: primary membranous nephropathy.
- APRIL and BAFF: two immune signaling proteins that pove blocks. "Anti-APRIL" drugs block only APRIL.
- CF: cystic fibrosis, Vertex's main franchise. YE: year-end.
- GAAP: US generally accepted accounting principles. Non-GAAP figures exclude items the company treats as one-time.
- R&D: research and development. IPR&D: acquired in-process R&D. SG&A: selling, general, and administrative expense. EPS: earnings per share.

- "We maintain a DCF to 2035 (8% discount rate; 2% terminal growth rate)" (p.1).

### Population definitions
- **Primary AMKD, US plus EU.** "The primary AMKD population represents a TAM of ~150K across US/ EU" (p.2).
- **Share of the broader market.** The primary population "reflects only ~50% of the broader TAM" (p.2).
- **Comorbid AMKD, US plus EU.** Truist writes that APOL1 inhibition's applicability "in the broader comorbid AMKD population (reflects another ~150K patients in US/EU) remains to be seen" (p.2). Adding the two figures gives about 300K US-plus-EU patients (calculated).
- **AMPLIFIED populations.** Truist describes the Phase 2 data as coming from AMKD patients "with less severe proteinuria and/ or comorbid diabetes" (p.2). Positive data "would essentially double the TAM" and represent "potential upside to our model" (p.2).
- **Diabetic versus non-diabetic.** Truist groups diabetic patients inside the "comorbid" population and gives no separate diabetic count (p.2).

## 3. Povetacicept and the kidney business
- **Filing and review.** Truist writes that "VRTX submitted its filing for accelerated approval in IgAN on 3/31/26 using a PRV" (p.1). It anticipates "BLA acceptance by ~May 29, 2026, followed by a priority review and a likely PDUFA date by late Sept. 2026" (p.1). Truist adds: "We see little risk to approval" (p.1).
- **Launch timing.** Truist expects the "approval/ launch of povetacicept for IgAN (expected by Fall 2026)" (p.1) and "the anticipated launch of povetacicept around September 2026" (p.3).
- **Sales.** Truist models pove 2035 revenue of "~$4.1B vs firm's prior PoS-adj (85%) estimate of $2.1B" (p.1). The new figure carries no PoS label. Expansion into pMN and other indications "represents potential upside" (p.1).
- **Model line (p.5).** Pove revenue is $6.9M in 4Q26E and 2026E, $200.8M in 2027E ($21.6M, $35.4M, $56.8M, and $87.0M by quarter), $601.1M in 2028E, $1,043.8M in 2029E, $1,730.5M in 2030E, $2,252.3M in 2031E, $2,822.7M in 2032E, $3,330.9M in 2033E, $3,795.6M in 2034E, and $4,142.8M in 2035E.
- **Pricing.** Truist writes that the IgAN market "could reach >$10-$20B" and that "some have questioned whether the ~$390K/year price point would prohibit adoption" (p.1). The report does not name the product that carries the ~$390K price.
- **Competitor launch.** Otsuka's anti-APRIL antibody Voyxact "achieved ~$29.1M in IgAN revenues in its first full quarter of launch" after accelerated approval in November 2025 (p.1). Otsuka received 880 new patient start forms in 1Q26, up from 180 in the partial 4Q25, for a total of 1,060 (p.1).
- **Profile and safety.** Truist cites pove's "convenient once-monthly injectable dosing" and its dual APRIL/BAFF mechanism (p.2). It names hypogammaglobulinemia (low antibody levels) and infection risk as "the primary potential barrier to broad adoption relative to anti-APRIL monotherapies" (p.2). Vertex "has indicated no observed signals of infection related to hypogammaglobulinemia to date" (p.2).
- **Prescribers.** Truist writes that "nephrologists appear receptive to the theoretical advantages of dual pathway inhibition" (p.2). The report does not discuss a sales force.
- **Other kidney programs.** The company description lists IgA nephropathy, APOL1-mediated kidney disease, primary membranous nephropathy, and autosomal dominant polycystic kidney disease among Vertex's pipeline areas (p.9).
- **Maze Therapeutics.** Truist writes that the APOL1 MoA "has been validated in this subset of patients by both VRTX and competitor Maze (MAZE, Buy)" (p.2). The sentence before it in the report describes the primary AMKD population. It adds that Maze "reported a small dataset across the variety of AMKD subgroups, notably showing mixed and difficult to interpret data in comorbid diabetic AMKD" (p.2). Truist rates Maze Buy at $26.35, with Danielle Brill as analyst (p.9). The report does not name MZE829 or give Maze trial numbers.

## 4. Company-level figures
- **2025 revenue.** Total revenue was $12,001.3M and product revenue $11,970.6M in 2025A (p.5). The 2025A product lines are Trikafta/Kaftrio $10,312.7M, Alyftrek $837.8M, other CF $644.7M, Journavx $59.6M, and Casgevy $115.8M (p.5).
- **2026 guidance.** The May 2026 guidance exhibit lists total revenue of $12,950M to $13,100M and non-CF revenue of "$500M or greater" (p.4). It lists GAAP R&D, IPR&D, and SG&A of $6,300M to $6,450M, and the non-GAAP equivalent of $5,650M to $5,750M (p.4). It also lists stock-based compensation of $650M to $700M, IPR&D of $95M to $105M, and a non-GAAP effective tax rate of 19.5% to 20.5% (p.4).
- **CF guidance.** The report cites "FY26 CF sales guidance of ~$12.45B to $12.6B" (p.2).
- **CF share of revenue.** The report does not state a share. CF products (Trikafta/Kaftrio, Alyftrek, other CF) sum to $11,795.2M, which is 98.3% of 2025A total revenue of $12,001.3M (calculated from p.5).
- **CF patients.** Management said in February 2026 that "over 77,000 patients are now on therapy (+2,000 vs. YE25)," with about 800 more US patients newly eligible as of April (p.2). About 30,000 to 33,000 eligible CF patients worldwide remain untreated (p.2).
- **Truist estimates.** 2026E product revenue is $13,106.6M, against a consensus of $13,228.2M (p.4). 2035E total revenue is $23,609.9M (p.5, p.8), described as "$23.6B vs firm's prior estimates of $24.2B" (p.1). CF 2035 sales are "~$15.4B" against a prior $15B, and Journavx 2035 sales are "~$1.7B" against a prior $2.9B (p.1). The thesis projects ">$15B in revenues for the CF portfolio at peak" (p.9).
- **EPS.** Non-GAAP EPS is $18.40 in 2025A, $19.43 in 2026E, and $21.77 in 2027E (p.5).
- **Market data.** The cover lists market capitalization of $111,600M, EV of $98,604M, cash and equivalents of $12,997M, total debt of $0, and 256M shares outstanding (p.1).
- **Exclusivity.** The report gives no exclusivity or patent dates.
- **Casgevy.** Truist cites "the 60/40 profit-split with CRISPR" (p.2).

## 5. Risks, and the view on AMKD efficacy outside APOL1-FSGS
- **Named risks.** The report names these risks: "Competition/ negative impacts to the Cystic Fibrosis (CF) base business, clinical trial failure of pipeline products and weaker adoption of new product launches" (p.9).
- **CF competitor.** Truist names the Sionna Therapeutics Phase 2 data, "due by mid-26," as "the latest overhang on the stock" (p.1).
- **Journavx.** Truist writes: "Given the lack of meaningful separation versus pregabalin in Phase 2, we expect a similar outcome in Phase 3" in diabetic peripheral neuropathy (p.2).
- **Pove safety.** Truist names hypogammaglobulinemia and infection risk as the main potential barrier to pove adoption against anti-APRIL drugs (p.2).
- **View on AMKD efficacy outside APOL1-FSGS.** Truist does not separate APOL1-FSGS from other primary AMKD. It calls the APOL1 mechanism "validated in this subset of patients" in a passage about the primary AMKD population (p.2). It says applicability "in the broader comorbid AMKD population ... remains to be seen" (p.2). It calls the comorbid result "not critical for inaxaplin's commercial success in our view" (p.2).
- **Section heading.** Page 2 heads the inaxaplin discussion "Inaxaplin represents an underappreciated potential blockbuster asset for AMKD" (p.2).
