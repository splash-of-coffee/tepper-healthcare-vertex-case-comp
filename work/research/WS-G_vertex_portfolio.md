# WS-G: Vertex Portfolio, Updated to Sept 27, 2026

Written 2026-09-27 for the Tepper Healthcare Case Competition team (research stream WS-G). This file covers Vertex's approved products, pipeline, revenue mix, field forces, kidney programs, milestone calendar, and deals. The file ends with what the portfolio implies for the AMKD primary care decision.

How to read the evidence tags:
- Source codes (S1 to S52) point to the "Sources" list near the end of this file. Each code carries a URL or a report name with its PDF page.
- Confidence: **high** means a company release, SEC filing, FDA database, or trial registry read directly. **Medium** means trade press, a machine transcript of a company call, or a search-engine summary of a primary page. **Low** means automated or untraceable aggregator text.
- Labels: **reported**, **company guidance**, **analyst expectation**, **team projection**, **estimate** (a number derived by WS-G, with its reasoning), and **inference** (a conclusion drawn from facts that no source states).
- "M" means millions of US dollars and "B" means billions.

## Summary

- **Cystic fibrosis (CF) drugs produced $11.80B of Vertex's $12.00B revenue in 2025 (98.3%).** Vertex's Aug 3, 2026 guidance of $13.1B to $13.2B, with at least $0.5B from non-CF drugs, caps CF at 96.2% of 2026 revenue before Crinetics (S1, S3; high).
- **Vertex's kidney field force is fully hired, and about 90% of its reps have nephrology experience.** The force covers nephrologists who see about 80% of US IgA nephropathy (IgAN) patients (S16, S17; medium-high). Povetacicept, due for an FDA decision on Nov 30, 2026, will be the third new APRIL-pathway IgAN drug in 12 months, after Voyxact (Nov 25, 2025) and Trutakna (Jul 7, 2026) (S15; high). Moving kidney reps toward primary care in 2027 (retargeting, Option 1c) would cut nephrologist calls during the povetacicept launch (inference).
- **Vertex has no primary care field force in any of its five business lines.** Its only late-stage program aimed at a primary-care-treated disease is suzetrigine for diabetic peripheral neuropathy (DPN; about 2.5M US patients; data about mid-2027) (S4, S21, S23). Vertex places the DPN program inside its "specialty commercial model" and its 300-rep Journavx force (S16, S17, S21; medium). A hired primary care team (Option 1a) would therefore have no second Vertex product to carry before 2028 (inference).
- **Vertex added 150 Journavx reps in one quarter (Q1 2026), "slightly ahead of plan."** It already runs consumer marketing for Journavx, including a connected-TV pilot since January 2026 and the Informed Pain Care telehealth site (S17, S18; medium-high). Hiring speed and consumer-ad experience do not block a contracted primary care force (Option 1b) plus added testing ads (Option 2) (inference).
- **Two 2027 readouts decide whether Vertex needs lasting primary care reps.** They are the AMPLITUDE interim analysis (early 2027) and the suzetrigine DPN Phase 3 trials (registry completion Apr 6 and May 31, 2027) (S8, S23; high). Povetacicept went from interim readout to FDA decision date in 8.7 months (Mar 10 to Nov 30, 2026), so Q4 2027 is the earliest plausible inaxaplin decision (team projection).

## Terms used in this file

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| AMKD | APOL1-mediated kidney disease: kidney disease caused by carrying two high-risk copies of the APOL1 gene. |
| CF | Cystic fibrosis, a genetic lung and digestive disease. CFTR is the faulty protein; CFTR modulators are Vertex's CF pills. |
| IgAN / pMN / ADPKD | IgA nephropathy and primary membranous nephropathy (two autoimmune kidney diseases); autosomal dominant polycystic kidney disease (inherited kidney cysts). |
| DPN / PNP / LSR | Diabetic peripheral neuropathy (nerve pain from diabetes); peripheral neuropathic pain (the wider nerve-pain category); lumbosacral radiculopathy (sciatica-type nerve pain). |
| CAH / ADCS | Congenital adrenal hyperplasia (inherited adrenal hormone disorder); ACTH-dependent Cushing's syndrome (excess cortisol driven by the hormone ACTH). |
| SCD / TDT | Sickle cell disease; transfusion-dependent beta thalassemia. Casgevy treats both. |
| T1D / T2D / DM1 / gMG / wAIHA | Type 1 diabetes; type 2 diabetes; myotonic dystrophy type 1 (genetic muscle disease); generalized myasthenia gravis (autoimmune nerve-muscle disease); warm autoimmune hemolytic anemia (autoimmune destruction of red blood cells). |
| BAFF / APRIL | Two immune signaling proteins that drive antibody production. Povetacicept and Trutakna block both; Voyxact blocks APRIL. |
| NDA / BLA / sNDA | New Drug Application (small molecules); Biologics License Application (biologics); supplemental NDA (a new use for an approved drug). |
| PDUFA date | The FDA's target date to decide on an application. |
| Accelerated approval | FDA approval based on an early marker (here, proteinuria or one-year kidney function), with confirmatory data required later. |
| Interim analysis (IA) | A planned early look at trial data. |
| eGFR / UPCR / UACR / htTKV | Estimated glomerular filtration rate (kidney filtering, from a blood test); urine protein-to-creatinine and albumin-to-creatinine ratios (urine protein leakage); height-adjusted total kidney volume (ADPKD cyst burden). |
| NCE exclusivity | Five years of FDA protection from generics for a new chemical entity, counted from approval. |
| Orphan exclusivity / pediatric exclusivity | Seven years of FDA protection for a rare-disease approval; six extra months added to patents or exclusivity for completing FDA-requested pediatric studies. |
| Orange Book / Purple Book | FDA databases that list patents and exclusivity for small-molecule drugs (Orange Book) and biologics (Purple Book). |
| PRV / CNPV | Priority review voucher (cuts an FDA review from 10 to 6 months); Commissioner's National Priority Voucher (a newer FDA program; Casgevy's pediatric filing was approved 53 days after submission with one). |
| BTD | Breakthrough Therapy designation: an FDA status that allows more FDA meetings and a rolling submission. |
| ATC | Authorized treatment center: a hospital certified to give Casgevy. |
| PCP / HCP | Primary care physician; healthcare professional (physician, nurse practitioner, physician assistant). |
| CSO / FTE | Contract sales organization (rents trained reps); full-time employee. |
| MSL / KOL | Medical science liaison (scientific, non-sales staff allowed before approval); key opinion leader (influential physician expert). |
| DTC / DTP / CTV | Direct-to-consumer and direct-to-patient advertising; connected TV (streaming TV ads). |
| SG&A / non-GAAP | Selling, general, and administrative expense (sales force and advertising sit here); company-adjusted figures that exclude items such as stock-based pay. |
| Acquired IPR&D | Acquired in-process research and development: the expensed purchase price of pipeline assets bought in a deal. |
| TCE | T-cell engager: an antibody that directs T cells to attack target cells. |

Arithmetic:
1. Kalydeco, Orkambi, and Symdeko in 2025: Vertex's "other product revenues" of $820.1M include $115.8M of Casgevy and $59.6M of Journavx (S3, footnote). The CF remainder is $820.1M − $115.8M − $59.6M = $644.7M.
2. CF revenue in 2025: $10,312.7M + $837.8M + $644.7M = $11,795.2M.
3. CF share of 2025 revenue: $11,795.2M ÷ $12,001.3M = 98.3% of total revenue. The share of product revenue is $11,795.2M ÷ $11,970.6M = 98.5%.
4. CF share under 2026 guidance: CF revenue is at most total revenue minus $0.5B. The low end of guidance gives ($13.1B − $0.5B) ÷ $13.1B = 96.2%. The high end gives ($13.2B − $0.5B) ÷ $13.2B = 96.2%. CF is therefore at most 96.2% of guided 2026 revenue.
5. CF share in H1 2026: $6,122.9M ÷ $6,320.8M = 96.9%.
6. Non-CF revenue needed in H2 2026 to meet guidance: $500M − $197.9M (H1 Casgevy $119.3M plus Journavx $78.6M) = $302.1M, or $151.1M per quarter. Q2 2026 non-CF revenue was $126.0M ($76.4M + $49.6M), so H2 must average 19.9% above the Q2 level.
7. Growth: the guidance midpoint of $13.15B is 9.6% above 2025 revenue of $12.00B. The implied CF midpoint of $12.65B is 7.2% above 2025 CF revenue of $11.80B.
8. Mix shift inside CF: Alyftrek rose from 7.0% of 2025 revenue to 15.8% of H1 2026 revenue as patients switched from Trikafta. Trikafta's Q2 2026 revenue fell 2.1% from Q2 2025 ($2,497.2M against $2,551.1M) (S1).
9. Crinetics: Vertex will update 2026 guidance with Q3 results on Nov 2, 2026 (S6). The $25M to $60M estimate for Palsonify from Sep to Dec 2026 equals 0.2% to 0.5% of the $13.15B midpoint (estimate). The low end uses the July consensus of about $70M for full-year 2026 (S22): $70M minus H1 revenue of $34.3M (S9) leaves about $36M for H2, or about $24M for four months. The high end assumes quarterly revenue grows from $24.0M in Q2 to about $50M in Q4.
10. Scale reference for option costs: Vertex's Q2 2026 non-GAAP SG&A was $520.2M, 44.7% above Q2 2025's $359.4M, which Vertex attributes to the Journavx launch and "the build-out of the renal franchise" (S1). H1 2026 non-GAAP SG&A of $952.4M annualizes to $1.90B. Each $50M a year of primary care spending would equal 2.6% of that base (arithmetic example; cost estimates belong to WS-C).

**Shared physicians.**
- All three kidney programs target US nephrologists. Vertex built the renal force "with the breadth of our renal pipeline in mind" (S16), so the same reps will promote inaxaplin to nephrologists after povetacicept (inference).

**Timing conflicts between the povetacicept launch and inaxaplin pre-launch work.**
1. Dec 2026 to Dec 2027, povetacicept launch year one. 2. Early 2027 to late 2027, inaxaplin pre-launch. The interim analysis arrives in early 2027 (S8). Filing, MSL education, payer preparation, and unbranded testing work would run through 2027 (team projection), in the same months as povetacicept's first year.

4. Oct 21 to 25, 2026, ASN Kidney Week in Denver (S28). The meeting falls five weeks before the povetacicept decision and overlaps the case final round on Oct 22 and 23.
5. Effect on the options. Retargeting kidney reps to primary care (Option 1c) takes nephrologist calls during the 12 to 18 months when two kidney launches need them (inference). A contracted primary care force (Option 1b) and added testing ads (Option 2) do not draw on the renal force (inference).

## 7. What the portfolio means for the AMKD primary care decision

The portfolio evidence supports the working hypothesis of a contracted primary care force plus added testing ads, staged to the 2027 readouts. A hired primary care team (Option 1a) would be Vertex's first primary care force in any of its five business lines. Vertex calls its approach a "specialty commercial model" (S16). Its investor relations team said a primary care pain market "could require a commercial partner" (S21). No second Vertex product needs primary care reps before 2028, because suzetrigine's DPN data arrive around mid-2027 and would likely ride the 300-rep Journavx force (inference). A contracted force (Option 1b) matches Vertex's partner-first stance on primary care markets. The contracted force can start before the early-2027 interim analysis and shrink if the label narrows or Maze's MZE829 arrives sooner than the team projects (inference). Vertex added 150 reps in one quarter in 2026, so it could convert or enlarge a contracted force later without losing a launch year (S17; inference). Retargeting kidney reps (Option 1c) would pull calls from a force Vertex hired for the povetacicept launch, which faces two same-pathway drugs approved in November 2025 and July 2026 (S15, S16). The renal force's 2027 time is committed to the povetacicept launch and to inaxaplin's nephrology pre-launch work, so retargeting would weaken both kidney launches (inference). Added direct-to-patient testing ads (Option 2) extend Power Forward and the free APOL1 testing program (S51), plus Vertex's Journavx consumer tools, which include connected TV and a telehealth site (S17, S18). The testing ads draw on no field force, so Vertex can run them as unbranded testing promotion before approval (inference; WS-D and WS-H to confirm the rules). The portfolio evidence weakens the hired team (1a) and retargeting (1c), and it supports the contracted force (1b) plus testing ads (2), scaled up at the interim analysis and again at approval.
